Eliminate, don't build.

The #1 move of every perfect scorer: hunt for what's wrong in each choice instead of constructing the 3D object in your head. Confirm-by-disproof beats mental modeling on nearly every type.

Answer everything.

No penalty for guessing. Never leave a blank. On a stuck item, take your elimination-narrowed best guess, mark it, and move — a 20-second solve and a 2-minute solve are both one point.

Two orders only.

Keyholes

Apertures · items 1–15aim 45sDrill
The rule + the method
One opening lets the whole object pass straight through. You may turn the object any way before entry, but not during — so the aperture is the object's exact silhouette from one side, to scale (and its inversion — passage runs either direction). Don't picture the solid; picture the flat shadow it casts. Test the three projections (front-back, top-bottom, side-side) + inversions, and cut any opening off in even one dimension. The whole widest cross-section must clear.
The traps
  • Wrong proportion / scale — right shape, subtly-off width or ratio. Most common, hardest. Judge internal length ratios, not the footprint.
  • Extra or missing notch — a feature added or dropped. Cheap to catch: notch-count first.
  • Mirror image — for keyholes a mirror can be a valid inversion; only wrong when it matches no real projection.
  • Internal-feature illusion — an interior line the outline never had.

Top-Front-End

View recognition · items 16–30aim 45sDrill
The rule + the method
Solid line = an edge you can see; dashed line = a hidden edge. Two views are given, pick the third. Reconstruct the solid, then project — don't guess line shapes, and don't line-count (the ADA killed that shortcut). Use the dimension-sharing rule: width = Top + Front, height = Front + End, depth = Top + End. Pre-decide which edges are solid vs dashed, then cut every choice with one wrong line.
The traps
  • Solid↔dashed swap — the signature trap; one visible/hidden line flipped kills the choice.
  • Extra or missing line — a feature added or a real edge omitted.
  • Mirrored / depth-flipped view — here a mirror is always wrong (views are direction-fixed).
  • Feature off-by-one — a hole or step shifted to the wrong edge; and sloped surfaces hiding as flat rectangles.

Angle Ranking

Angle discrimination · items 31–45aim 20sDrill
The rule + the method
Rank four angles smallest → largest. Ray length lies — the angle lives at the vertex. Anchor the obvious smallest and largest first (that alone eliminates orderings), then argue only the close middle pair. Trust your first read and move — extra time here does not raise accuracy. ~20 seconds, gut-shot.
The traps
  • Long rays look bigger — the #1 distortion; trim every angle's arms to equal length in your mind.
  • Rotation — angles turned to defeat side-by-side comparison.
  • Near-equal middle pair — ~1–3° apart; over-thinking flips the answer.
  • Reading the exterior angle instead of the interior one.

Hole Punching

Paper folding · items 46–60aim 22sDrill
The rule + the method
The Grid Method: draw the 4×4 grid (16 possible holes), mark the punch in the final fold, then unfold one crease at a time in reverse, mirroring every hole across that crease — a fold line is a line of symmetry. Each fold over the punch doubles the holes, so N folds → up to 2N. Fast check: the answer must be symmetric about the first fold's line.
The traps
  • Forgot to mirror a fold — too few holes.
  • Wrong axis — mirrored across horizontal vs vertical vs diagonal; the whole-answer mirror image.
  • Missing / extra hole — miscounting the layers.
  • Off-by-one cell — a hole in the adjacent grid square.

Cube Counting

items 61–75aim 25sDrill
The rule + the method
The figure is painted on every exposed face except the bottom it rests on, and faces cemented to a neighbor are never painted — so no cube ever has more than 5 painted faces. Sweep once, one direction (bottom→top, front→back, left→right — bottom first, that's where hidden cubes hide), tally each cube 0–5, then verify: total tally = total cubes.
The traps
  • Painting the resting bottom face — the single most-misapplied rule.
  • Hidden support cubes forgotten — they count, and are the usual source of the 0-painted answers.
  • Shared faces counted as painted — a face touching another cube gets no paint.
  • A "6 painted faces" option — always impossible; and the back column mis-counted.

Pattern Folding

3D form development · items 76–90aim 50sDrill
The rule + the method
Fold INTO the page, never out — folding outward gives the mirror image, the built-in trap. Anchor one distinctive face, fold its two neighbors, eliminate — one landmark at a time, don't fold the whole net. Faces sharing an edge become adjacent; opposite faces never touch and never both appear in one view. For shaded/arrow faces, check position and rotation.
The traps
  • Mirror-image figure — a reflection no rotation can make (the fold-outward result).
  • One face rotated — right symbol, wrong direction; an arrow pointing to the wrong neighbor.
  • Wrong-neighbor adjacency — two faces shown touching that aren't adjacent in the net.
  • Opposite faces both visible — impossible in a single view.
