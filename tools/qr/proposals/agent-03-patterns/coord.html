Coordinate geometry — perpendicular slopes are negative reciprocals

The trap: thinking the negative reciprocal means just adding a minus sign, or computing slope by reversing y and x in the difference (run-over-rise instead of rise-over-run).

Canonical Trap

Pick the setup, not the answer

One inverts the slope correctly. The other only flips the sign.
DAT-style problem
Line L has slope 2/3. What is the slope of any line perpendicular to L?
The trap pattern
Perpendicular slopes multiply to −1.

If you only flip the sign of 2/3 to −2/3, the two lines are not perpendicular — they cross at an angle, but not 90°. Check: (2/3)(−2/3) = −4/9, not −1.

The product rule is the safe one: m·m⊥ = −1. So m⊥ = −1/m. For m = 2/3, that is −3/2. For m = −5, that is 1/5.

Lock-line: Perpendicular slope = negative reciprocal. Flip the fraction and swap the sign.

Drill the recognition

3 problems · same trap, different numbers
A line has slope −1/4. Slope of perpendicular line?
Negative reciprocal of −1/4 is +4. Check: (−1/4)(4) = −1.
A line passes through (1,2) and (5,10). Slope of perpendicular?
Slope = (10−2)/(5−1) = 2. Negative reciprocal: −1/2.
A line has slope 0 (horizontal). Perpendicular slope?
Horizontal and vertical are perpendicular. Reciprocal of 0 is undefined — that is the vertical line.
