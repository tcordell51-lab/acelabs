PATTERN PATH · WORK-RATE

Work-rate — add rates, not times

The trap: averaging the two completion times instead of adding the rates. Time is what you get back at the end — the workers add their work-per-hour, not their hours.

Canonical Trap
W

Pick the setup, not the answer

One averages the times. The other adds the rates and inverts at the end.
DAT-style problem
Pump A fills a tank in 4 hours. Pump B fills the same tank in 6 hours. Working together, how long do they take?
The trap pattern
Two pumps together must be faster than either alone.

Sanity check: Pump A alone is 4 hours. Adding a second pump should take less than 4 hours, never more. The averaged-time setup gives 5 hours — impossible.

Rates are work-per-hour. They add directly because both pumps are pouring at the same time. Then invert: time = 1 / (sum of rates).

Lock-line: ttogether = 1 ÷ (1/tA + 1/tB). Or shortcut for two: (tA·tB) / (tA + tB).

Drill the recognition

3 problems · same trap, different numbers
A bakes in 3 h, B bakes in 6 h. Together?
1/3 + 1/6 = 1/2, so t = 2 h. The shortcut: (3·6)/(3+6) = 18/9 = 2.
Hose 1 in 10 min, Hose 2 in 15 min. Together?
1/10 + 1/15 = 5/30 = 1/6, so t = 6 min.
Worker A in 8 h, identical worker B. Together?
1/8 + 1/8 = 1/4, so 4 h. Two identical workers always halve the time.
