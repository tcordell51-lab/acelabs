Pick the setup, not the answer

Tap Setup A or Setup B. Right or wrong, you'll see the trap pattern named.
DAT-style problem
30 mL of 10% saline is mixed with 70 mL of 50% saline. What is the concentration of the resulting solution?
The trap pattern
Concentrations don't average — they weight.

If you have more of the 50% than the 10%, the final concentration must lean toward 50%. A flat average treats both bottles like they're the same size. They're not.

Think of it physically: the bigger jug pulls the answer toward itself. 30 mL of 10% contributes 3 grams of solute. 70 mL of 50% contributes 35 grams. Total: 38 g in 100 mL = 38%.

Lock-line: total solute ÷ total volume. The numerator is (V₁·C₁ + V₂·C₂), never (C₁+C₂)/2.

Drill the recognition

3 problems · same trap, different numbers
40 mL of 20% acid mixed with 60 mL of 70% acid. Final concentration?
Volumes weighted: (8 + 42)/100 = 50%. The flat average gives 45 — close enough to feel right, which is exactly why this trap works on test day.
200 mL of 15% sugar mixed with 50 mL of 60% sugar. Final concentration?
200·0.15 = 30, 50·0.6 = 30. Total solute 60 in 250 mL = 24%. The mixture leans toward 15% because there's 4× more of it.
10 mL of 40% bleach mixed with 90 mL of pure water (0%). Final concentration?
Diluting into a much bigger volume of zero-percent water drags the concentration way down. 4 grams in 100 mL = 4%. Averaging would give 20% — five times too high.
