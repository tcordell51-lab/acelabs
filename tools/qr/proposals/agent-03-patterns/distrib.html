Distribution — every term, every time

The trap: distributing the multiplier to only the first term inside the parentheses, or forgetting the sign on the second term when there is a minus out front.

Pick the setup, not the answer

One option distributes to every term. The other distributes to one and skips the rest.
DAT-style problem
Simplify: 3(2x − 5) − 2(x + 4)
The trap pattern
Distribution is plural. The multiplier hits every term.

The trap setup pretends only the first term inside the parentheses gets multiplied. On the DAT, the answer choices will include the partial-distribution result so it feels right.

Even worse with a leading minus: −2(x+4) means −2x − 8, not −2x + 4. The minus is part of the multiplier — it travels with the 2.

Lock-line: Write the multiplier on the outside, draw arrows to every term, then collect.

Drill the recognition

3 problems · same trap, different numbers
Simplify 4(x − 3) − (2x + 1)
4x − 12 − 2x − 1 = 2x − 13. The lone minus on the second group flips both terms.
Expand −3(2y + 5) + 2(y − 1)
−6y − 15 + 2y − 2 = −4y − 17. The −3 multiplies both 2y and +5.
Simplify 5 − 2(3x − 4)
5 − 6x + 8 = −6x + 13. The −2 turns −4 into +8 because two negatives multiply to a positive.
