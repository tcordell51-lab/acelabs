Supplementary · rare-but-tested

Bonus topics.

The six hubs cover the spine of DAT Bio. These nine topics show up in maybe one or two questions per test — not enough to build a hub around, too important to skip. Skim these the week of test day.

Cell · biotech

CRISPR & gene editing

Cas9 nuclease guided by a single guide RNA (sgRNA) cuts double-stranded DNA at a complementary target. The cell repairs the cut by NHEJ (error-prone, knocks out genes) or HDR (template-driven, edits genes).

Why it matters: shows up as the modern parallel to restriction enzymes. Know sgRNA = address, Cas9 = scissors.
  • Cas9 = blunt-cut endonuclease
  • PAM (NGG for SpCas9) is the recognition signal upstream of cut
  • NHEJ for knockout, HDR + template for knock-in
Genetics · chromosome anomalies

Aneuploidies & nondisjunction

Failure of homologs (meiosis I) or sister chromatids (meiosis II) to separate produces gametes with extra or missing chromosomes. Most aneuploidies are lethal; viable ones cluster on small chromosomes and sex chromosomes.

Why it matters: trisomy 21 (Down), XXY (Klinefelter), XO (Turner) are stock DAT examples — know mechanism + phenotype.
  • Meiosis I error → all four gametes affected
  • Meiosis II error → two of four gametes affected
  • Maternal age increases nondisjunction risk
Cell · communication

Apoptosis vs. necrosis

Apoptosis is programmed: caspases activate, the cell shrinks, DNA fragments into 200 bp ladders, membrane blebs but stays intact. Necrosis is unprogrammed: membrane ruptures, contents spill, inflammation follows.

Why it matters: trap question contrasts the two. Apoptosis = clean. Necrosis = messy.
  • Intrinsic path: mitochondrial cytochrome c release
  • Extrinsic path: death-receptor (Fas/FasL) signaling
  • Bcl-2 inhibits, Bax promotes
Immunology · cell defense

Innate vs. adaptive immunity

Innate is fast and generic: physical barriers, phagocytes, complement, NK cells. Adaptive is slow but specific: B cells (antibodies, humoral) and T cells (cytotoxic + helper, cell-mediated). Memory cells make secondary responses faster and stronger.

Why it matters: DAT loves "which cell does what" matching. Lock the four T-cell + two B-cell flavors.
  • MHC I = all nucleated cells, presents to CD8
  • MHC II = APCs (dendritic, macrophage, B), presents to CD4
  • Vaccines exploit memory B/T cells
Microbiology · viral

Viral life cycles

Lytic: virus replicates, lyses host. Lysogenic: viral DNA integrates as a prophage, replicates with host until induction triggers lytic switch. Retroviruses use reverse transcriptase to write RNA → DNA into the host genome.

Why it matters: HIV's RT and integrase are stock DAT material. So is bacteriophage λ.
  • Lytic: rapid, kills host
  • Lysogenic: latent, no symptoms
  • Retrovirus: ssRNA(+), reverse transcribes
Cell · cycle control

Cell-cycle checkpoints

G1/S checks size + DNA damage (p53 gates this — its loss is in >50% of cancers). G2/M checks DNA replication completion. M (spindle) checks kinetochore attachment before anaphase. Cyclin/CDK complexes drive each transition.

Why it matters: cancer questions inevitably test "what does p53 do" and "which cyclin pairs with which CDK".
  • Cyclin D + CDK4/6 → G1
  • Cyclin E + CDK2 → G1/S
  • Cyclin B + CDK1 → G2/M
Genetics · population

Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium

p² + 2pq + q² = 1 holds when there's no mutation, no selection, no migration, no drift, and random mating. Deviations from those five conditions are how populations evolve.

Why it matters: any "what fraction of the population are carriers?" question wants 2pq.
  • p + q = 1 for two alleles
  • q² = homozygous recessive frequency (often the only thing you can measure)
  • 2pq = heterozygote (carrier) frequency
Ecology · energy flow

Trophic levels & energy transfer

~10% of energy passes from one trophic level to the next; the rest is lost as heat. Pyramids of energy never invert. Pyramids of biomass and numbers can (think parasites).

Why it matters: limits on food-chain length come from this. Top predators are rare for thermodynamic reasons.
  • Producers → primary consumers → secondary → tertiary
  • 10% rule = approximate; actual range 5–20%
  • Energy flows; nutrients cycle
Plants · the lone botany topic

Plant basics

The DAT does not test botany deeply, but expect one question on photosynthesis (covered in Cell), C3 vs. C4 vs. CAM adaptations, or angiosperm life cycle (alternation of generations: gametophyte → sporophyte).

Why it matters: punctuated equilibrium — read this once, move on. Diminishing returns are real.
  • C4: spatial separation of carbon fixation (corn, sugarcane)
  • CAM: temporal separation (cacti, succulents)
  • Angiosperms: dominant sporophyte, reduced gametophyte
