Germ layer derivatives

Three primary germ layers form during gastrulation. Each gives rise to specific adult tissues. The DAT tests "which germ layer makes [adult structure]?" repeatedly.

OUTER LAYER
Ectoderm
  • Epidermis of skin (+ hair, nails, sweat glands)
  • Entire nervous system — brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves
  • Adrenal medulla (neural crest → catecholamines)
  • Eye lens, cornea, retina
  • Tooth enamel
  • Anterior pituitary (Rathke's pouch)
  • Inner ear epithelium
  • Neural crest derivatives: melanocytes, Schwann cells, facial cartilage
MIDDLE LAYER
Mesoderm
  • All muscle — skeletal, smooth, cardiac
  • Bone + cartilage
  • Dermis of skin
  • Circulatory system — heart, vessels, blood
  • Lymphatic system
  • Kidneys + ureters
  • Gonads (ovaries, testes)
  • Adrenal cortex (gotcha! medulla is ectoderm)
  • Notochord, somites, dentin
INNER LAYER
Endoderm
  • GI tract epithelial lining (NOT mouth/anus — those are ectoderm)
  • Respiratory tract lining
  • Liver
  • Pancreas
  • Thyroid + parathyroid
  • Thymus
  • Bladder + urethra lining
  • Auditory tube
High-yield DAT trap items: Adrenal medulla = ectoderm (neural crest), adrenal cortex = mesoderm. Mouth + anus epithelium = ectoderm (NOT endoderm — they invaginated from outside). Lens of eye = ectoderm. Notochord = mesoderm.

Memorize the layers
Mnemonics

Ecto = "ekto" = OUTSIDE. Skin (outside) + nervous system (came from skin invagination during neurulation).

Meso = MIDDLE. Muscle, bone, blood — the middle stuff. "Mesoderm = meat-o-derm."

Endo = INSIDE. Lining of tubes that connect to the inside (GI, lungs).

Diploblastic vs triploblastic: Cnidarians have only 2 layers (ecto + endo). All bilaterians have 3 (mesoderm added) → enables true muscles + organs.

Protostomes vs Deuterostomes

The first major split among bilaterians. Determines mouth/anus origin, cleavage type, coelom formation, embryonic plasticity.

Extraembryonic membranes (amniotes)
Amnion

Fluid-filled sac surrounding embryo. Cushions + protects from desiccation + impact.

Chorion

Outermost membrane. Gas exchange. In mammals, contributes fetal portion of placenta.

Allantois

Waste storage (uric acid in birds/reptiles). In mammals, becomes umbilical cord vessels.

Yolk sac

Nutrition in egg-layers. In mammals: first site of hematopoiesis + germ cell origin.

Inducers + morphogens (DAT-tested names)
Spemann's Organizer

Dorsal lip of blastopore in frog. Induces overlying ectoderm to form neural plate. Classical "organizer" experiment.

Notochord

Mesodermal rod. Induces overlying ectoderm to form neural tube during neurulation.

Sonic Hedgehog (Shh)

Morphogen for limb + neural tube patterning. Concentration gradient → positional info.

Hox genes

Master regulators of anterior-posterior axis identity. Order on chromosome matches order of body segments they specify.

DAT shortcuts
Memory hooks

Proto = first: blastopore opens first → becomes the FIRST opening = mouth.

Deutero = second: mouth opens second; the blastopore becomes the anus.

Schizocoely = SPLIT: protostomes split open mesoderm to form coelom.

Enterocoely = ENTER: deuterostomes enter the archenteron to pouch off mesoderm.

Identical twins only possible in deuterostomes — early cells are totipotent (indeterminate cleavage).

Section 05 · Developmental

Fertilization: contact → fast block → slow block.

Sea urchin model is DAT-canonical. Sequence: sperm contacts egg jelly → acrosomal reaction → fast block (Na+ depolarization) → cortical reaction / slow block (Ca²⁺ wave + fertilization envelope) → membrane fusion + activation → meiosis II completes.

The trap

Students confuse fast block (membrane depolarization, seconds) with slow block (cortical reaction, minutes). They miss that egg activation triggers the COMPLETION of meiosis II.

The rule

Fast block = Na+ influx → membrane depolarization (seconds, temporary). Slow block = Ca²⁺ wave → cortical granule fusion → fertilization envelope (minutes, permanent). Egg activation triggers meiosis II completion (was arrested in metaphase II).

The move

Identify the polyspermy block by timing (seconds = fast/electrical) vs (minutes = slow/cortical). Track meiotic arrest stages.

Fast block electrical. Slow block cortical. Activation finishes meiosis II.

From CoachCoach notes · the full sequence

Five steps. Each one matters and the DAT can ask about any of them.

1. Contact. Sperm encounters egg's outer coat. Sea urchin: jelly coat with species-specific receptors. Mammal: zona pellucida with ZP3 glycoprotein. Species specificity prevents cross-species fertilization.

2. Acrosomal reaction. Sperm acrosome (Golgi-derived vesicle at sperm tip) undergoes exocytosis. Hydrolytic enzymes (acrosin = trypsin-like) digest the jelly. Acrosomal process extends with bindin on tip → binds bindin receptor on egg vitelline envelope.

3. Fast block (seconds). Membrane depolarization: Na⁺ influx through voltage-gated channels takes membrane potential from −70 mV to ~+20 mV. Electrical block prevents 2nd sperm fusion. Temporary — membrane re-polarizes within minutes.

4. Slow block / cortical reaction (minutes). Ca²⁺ wave propagates from sperm-fusion site across egg. Triggers cortical granules (just under PM) to fuse with PM and release contents into perivitelline space. Fertilization envelope hardens around egg. Permanent block.

5. Egg activation. Same Ca²⁺ wave triggers metabolic activation: increased protein synthesis, DNA synthesis preparation. Releases meiotic arrest at metaphase II → meiosis II completes → mature ovum (n) + 2nd polar body. Sperm + egg pronuclei fuse → 2n zygote.

Common student misconceptions
  • "Fast block = cortical reaction." No — fast = depolarization (electrical, seconds). Cortical reaction = SLOW block (envelope hardening, minutes).
  • "Egg is haploid before fertilization." Tricky — the secondary oocyte is technically n with 2n DNA content (2 sister chromatids per chromosome). Meiosis II completes only AFTER fertilization, producing the truly haploid ovum.
  • "Acrosin is the egg's response." No — acrosin is a SPERM enzyme released from the acrosome to digest the egg coat.
See also Cell cycle + mitosis · meiosis II = sister chromatid separation, mechanically like mitosis.

Section 05 · Developmental

Cleavage + gastrulation: from zygote to three germ layers.

After fertilization: cleavage = rapid mitotic divisions WITHOUT growth → blastula (hollow ball). Gastrulation = cells migrate to form 3 germ layers: ectoderm (outer), mesoderm (middle), endoderm (inner). The blastopore forms at the site of invagination — its fate (mouth or anus) splits protostomes from deuterostomes. Spemann's organizer = inducer that defines body axes.

The trap

Cleavage divisions DON'T grow the embryo. Cells get smaller each round. Gastrulation rearranges existing cells — doesn't add new ones until later. The "primitive streak" in birds/mammals is functionally equivalent to amphibian dorsal lip / Spemann's organizer.

The rule

Cleavage: rapid M phases, no G1 or G2 growth. Result: morula → blastula. Gastrulation: 3 germ layers form. Ectoderm → skin, nervous system. Mesoderm → muscle, bone, blood, kidney, gonads. Endoderm → gut lining, lungs, liver, pancreas.

The move

For germ-layer Qs: outer (ecto) → skin/CNS. Middle (meso) → muscle/bone/blood. Inner (endo) → gut/lungs. For organizer Qs: dorsal lip in amphibians = primitive streak in amniotes.

Ecto = outer (skin, CNS). Meso = middle (muscle). Endo = inner (gut).

From CoachCoach notes · cleavage, blastula, gastrulation, organizer

Same DNA, three layers, every tissue.

Stage 1 — Fertilization: sperm + egg → zygote (2n). Cortical reaction blocks polyspermy.

Stage 2 — Cleavage: rapid mitoses without G1/G2 growth phases (cell cycle = S → M only). Cell number increases, total volume constant. Cells (blastomeres) get progressively smaller.

  • Holoblastic cleavage: entire egg divides (low yolk: amphibians, mammals).
  • Meroblastic cleavage: only part divides (high yolk: birds, fish, reptiles).
  • Spiral cleavage: protostomes (cells offset).
  • Radial cleavage: deuterostomes (cells stacked).

Stage 3 — Blastula: hollow ball of cells (~64-128 cells). Cavity = blastocoel. Mammals call this stage "blastocyst" with inner cell mass + trophoblast.

Stage 4 — Gastrulation: cells migrate to form three germ layers + a primitive gut (archenteron):

  • In amphibians: invagination begins at the dorsal lip of the blastopore (Spemann's organizer).
  • In birds + mammals: cells migrate through the primitive streak (functionally equivalent to dorsal lip).
  • The opening (blastopore) becomes the mouth (protostomes) or anus (deuterostomes).

Three germ layers and their derivatives:

  • Ectoderm (outer): epidermis, hair, nails, sweat glands, mammary glands; ENTIRE NERVOUS SYSTEM (brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves); neural crest derivatives (PNS ganglia, melanocytes, adrenal medulla, facial cartilage); lens of eye, cornea; tooth enamel; anterior pituitary.
  • Mesoderm (middle): all muscle (skeletal, smooth, cardiac); bone, cartilage, dermis; blood, blood vessels, heart; kidneys + ureters; gonads + reproductive tracts; spleen; adrenal cortex.
  • Endoderm (inner): EPITHELIAL LINING of GI tract; epithelial lining of respiratory tract; liver, pancreas, gallbladder; thyroid, parathyroid; bladder lining.

Spemann's organizer experiment (1924): transplanting the dorsal blastopore lip to another embryo induced a SECOND body axis. This showed the organizer secretes signals (we now know: noggin, chordin, Wnt antagonists) that pattern the body. Nobel Prize 1935.

Induction: one tissue secretes morphogens that change neighboring tissue's fate. Examples: notochord → induces overlying ectoderm to form neural plate.

Common student misconceptions
  • "Cleavage cells grow before each division." They DON'T grow — that's the defining feature. Cells get smaller each round.
  • "Heart is from endoderm." Heart is from MESODERM. Endoderm forms epithelial linings.
  • "Brain is from mesoderm." Entire nervous system is from ECTODERM (via neural plate → neural tube).
  • "Mammals don't have a primitive streak." They do — it's functionally equivalent to amphibian dorsal lip.

    Section 05 · Developmental

    Organogenesis: neural tube, notochord, neural crest.

    After gastrulation, organs form. Neurulation: notochord (mesoderm) induces overlying ectoderm to form the neural plate → folds into neural tube (CNS) + neural crest cells (PNS, melanocytes, adrenal medulla, facial cartilage). Neural tube defects (e.g., spina bifida) result from incomplete closure. Body plan finalizes via Hox genes.

    The trap

    Neural crest cells migrate widely and form a HUGE diversity of structures (PNS, melanocytes, adrenal medulla, parts of face, dental papilla). They are sometimes called the "fourth germ layer." Folic acid prevents neural tube closure defects (spina bifida, anencephaly).

    The rule

    Notochord induces neural plate. Neural plate → folds → neural tube. Neural tube → CNS (brain + spinal cord). Neural crest cells (separate from neural tube) → PNS, melanocytes, adrenal medulla, face. Hox genes set anteroposterior body axis.

    The move

    For organ-origin Qs: trace germ layer (see Cleavage + Gastrulation node), then induction step. For Hox: anterior genes 5' on cluster, posterior 3' (or reverse — memorize linear arrangement matches body).

    Neural tube → CNS. Neural crest → PNS + melanocytes + adrenal medulla.

    From CoachCoach notes · neurulation + organogenesis

    Specific cells, specific origins.

    Neurulation (forms the nervous system):

    • Notochord (mesoderm rod beneath dorsal ectoderm) secretes signals (Sonic Hedgehog).
    • Overlying ectoderm thickens into neural plate.
    • Plate edges (neural folds) rise → neural groove deepens → folds meet midline → neural tube closes.
    • Anterior end → brain (forebrain → midbrain → hindbrain).
    • Posterior tube → spinal cord.

    Neural tube defects: incomplete closure → anencephaly (anterior failure, fatal) or spina bifida (posterior failure). Folate (folic acid) supplementation in early pregnancy reduces risk.

    Neural crest cells: a population that DELAMINATES from the dorsal neural tube edges and migrates throughout the embryo. Sometimes called the "fourth germ layer" because of their derivative diversity:

    • Peripheral nervous system ganglia (DRG, autonomic).
    • Schwann cells.
    • Melanocytes (pigment).
    • Adrenal medulla (chromaffin cells).
    • Cartilage of face + parts of skull.
    • C cells of thyroid.
    • Dental papilla.
    • Aorticopulmonary septum (heart outflow tract).

    Body axes:

    • Anteroposterior (head→tail): Hox genes. Linearly arranged on chromosome; expression order matches body order (collinearity). Mutations → homeotic transformations (e.g., antennapedia in flies = leg where antenna should be).
    • Dorsoventral (back↔belly): BMP gradients. Notochord/Spemann's organizer secretes BMP antagonists (chordin, noggin) to make dorsal.
    • Left-right: nodal flow at primitive node creates asymmetry.

    Mesoderm subdivisions:

    • Notochord: (transient in vertebrates, persists in invertebrate chordates). Becomes nucleus pulposus of intervertebral disc in adults.
    • Paraxial mesoderm → somites: dermatome (dermis), myotome (skeletal muscle), sclerotome (vertebrae, ribs).
    • Intermediate mesoderm: kidneys + reproductive tract.
    • Lateral plate mesoderm: heart, blood, body wall.

    Apoptosis in development: finger separation requires programmed cell death of interdigital webbing. Failure → syndactyly (webbed fingers).

    Common student misconceptions
    • "Neural tube becomes the entire nervous system." Neural tube → CNS only. Neural CREST → PNS + many other structures.
    • "Adrenal cortex and medulla have the same origin." Cortex = mesoderm. Medulla = neural crest (essentially modified sympathetic neurons).
    • "Hox genes are mammal-specific." Highly conserved across all bilaterians (flies → humans share homologous Hox clusters).
    • "Webbed-finger separation is just from cell pulling apart." Apoptosis (programmed cell death) of interdigital cells. Failure = syndactyly.
