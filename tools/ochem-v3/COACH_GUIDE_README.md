## What these tools are

OChem in Ace Labs is split across two complementary engines:

- **OChem v3** (`tools/ochem-v3/index.html`) — the study path. 14 chapters, lesson-by-lesson, with mechanism animations, decision wizards, problem bank, and DAT-format mock. This is where students learn.
- **OChem v1 / Reaction Maps** (`tools/ochem/index.html`) — the reference encyclopedia. 11 hubs + 8 labs + 27 mechanism animations. Open-ended exploration of reaction families.

For coaching, **v3 is the primary engine**. The reaction maps are deep-dive support — useful for "let me look this up" moments mid-session, not the structured curriculum.

The coaching pedagogy follows the same cross-subject principles as QR/Bio/GC (see [ACELABS_COACH_GUIDE.md](../../ACELABS_COACH_GUIDE.md)), with one OChem-specific shift: the work is reaction-pattern recognition, not formula derivation. Students don't *compute* in OChem; they *recognize* and *predict*. The pedagogy emphasizes mechanism narration over numeric calculation.

## Three plans

Same structure as QR — 1-month crash, 2-month default, 3-month extra support. OChem plans differ from QR in important ways:

1. **Pattern recognition is the unit, not the formula.** Sessions are organized around mechanism families, not modules per se.
2. **Cumulative complexity.** Chapter 13 (aldol) requires Chapter 11 (carbonyl) requires Chapter 4 (acid/base). Sequencing matters more than in QR.
3. **No "test day" strategy modules separate from content.** The test-day moves (skip, eliminate, guess) get integrated into mechanism-prediction practice.

## OChem-specific coaching principles

In addition to the cross-subject principles in ACELABS_COACH_GUIDE:

### 1. Mechanism narration > memorization

Students who memorize "this reaction gives this product" plateau around 19. Students who narrate the mechanism aloud — "the nucleophile attacks here because…, the leaving group leaves because…, the proton transfer happens because…" — push to 22+. Make narration mandatory in worked examples.

### 2. Decision wizards before brute-force prediction

The SN1/SN2/E1/E2 decision tree is the highest-leverage coaching moment in all of OChem. The wizard in v3 walks through substrate / nucleophile / base / solvent. Use it explicitly for the first 2 weeks of the substitution/elimination block.

### 3. Reagent reference, not memorization

Don't ask students to memorize "what does PCC do?" — ask them to look it up the first 20 times in the Reagent Reference (in the v1 reaction maps). After 20 lookups, recall sticks. Memorization without application doesn't stick.

### 4. Spectroscopy late, not early

IR + NMR are the last chapter (Ch 14) for a reason. They make sense only after the structure work is solid. Don't try to teach spectroscopy in week 1 even if a student asks — they'll freeze. Bank a "looking forward to spectroscopy" anchor early in the program and deliver it as a reward in the final sessions.

### 5. Reaction maps as the encyclopedia, v3 as the curriculum

Treat v1 (reaction maps) as the textbook the student opens during sessions. Treat v3 as the curriculum that walks them forward. Don't try to teach from the maps alone — they're not designed for sequenced learning.
