What Ace Labs is.

Ace Labs is a supplemental DAT repair lab. It is not a primary DAT prep course. The intended workflow is: keep using your main DAT prep source (Bootcamp, Booster, DAT Destroyer, Anki, your tutor, or any combination), and when a Bio, Gen Chem, OChem, or QR question keeps tripping you up, log the miss here, work through the repair, drill similar items, then retest in your primary resource.

Ace Labs is built and maintained by AceTheDAT. Content is authored by AceTheDAT and revised against the current ADA outline. Reviewer credentials have not yet been published.

Practice tests · scope and limits.

3 fixed Prometric-style practice tests. 140 questions per test (40 Bio + 30 GC + 30 OChem + 40 QR). 135 minutes per test. Zero question overlap across the three — 420 unique questions in total.

What these tests are. Covered-section pacing simulations. Modeled on the Prometric testing interface for the sections Ace Labs has banks for: section timers, question navigation panel, mark-for-review, end-of-section review screen, periodic table reference for Gen Chem, and a basic on-screen calculator for QR.

What these tests are not. Full DAT simulations. The real DAT runs 5h 15m across 4 sections. Ace Labs covers about 2h 15m of seat time across 2 sections. The real DAT places its scheduled 30-minute break between PAT and Reading Comprehension — Ace Labs does not include that break, since it does not ship those sections.

Score language · practice estimates, not predictions.

The DAT moved from the legacy 1–30 score scale to the new 200–600 ADA scaled score on March 1, 2025. Ace Labs uses the 200–600 scale.

  • Practice estimate: a directional accuracy + pacing read, not a predicted DAT score. Use it to compare attempts against yourself, not to forecast your real score.
  • Not psychometrically equated. The ADA equates real DAT forms so a 21 means the same difficulty across forms. Ace Labs does not have the candidate-pool data to do that. Difficulty between Test 1, Test 2, and Test 3 is not statistically equated.
  • Covered-section accuracy proxy. Where Ace Labs displays a multi-section composite, it is a covered-section proxy — not the official DAT Academic Average, which is computed across all four scored sections.
  • No score prediction. Ace Labs does not predict your real DAT score and does not claim to.

Known gaps.

Things we know are missing or in progress, listed honestly:

  • Distractor-level diagnosis — not yet present in every engine. Gen Chem leads; Bio, OChem, and QR are catching up.
  • Miss → Repair → Retest workflow — in design. The ability to log a miss from another resource, generate a repair card, and mark it repaired only after a cold retest is on the immediate roadmap.
  • Cross-engine retrieval queue — partial. Each engine has its own spaced-repetition layer; the unified Today queue across all four is in design.
  • Mobile rendering at narrow widths — some pages overflow horizontally below 430px. Being fixed in a dedicated mobile pass.
  • Reviewer credentials — not yet published. Internal review is performed by AceTheDAT staff; outside reviewer attestation has not yet shipped.
  • 15-minute diagnostic — in design. The current diagnostic is a 60-question, 45-minute cross-section read.
