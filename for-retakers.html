Going again.
Let's make it count.

You've already sat in that room. You know what the pressure feels like. This time around, the work is different — narrower, more targeted, more honest about where the gaps actually are.

Section A

What you already know

A retake is not a reset. You have a real, scored official attempt to work from. That's the most honest data point anyone in DAT prep can have.

Most retakers walk in with more content knowledge than they credit themselves for. The first attempt surfaces which sections actually matter and which ones were already close. Use it.

  • Your official score report tells you which sections are worth the most ground to gain.
  • The sections you passed within a few points of your target need maintenance, not a full rebuild.
  • Sections far below target deserve focused, structured work — not just more practice questions.
  • Anxiety on test day is normal. It is also trainable. Timed mocks under realistic conditions are the only thing that actually moves that needle.
Section B

What's different this time

Ace Labs was built around the ways the DAT actually tests — not the way textbooks teach. For retakers specifically, that means a few things work in your favor:

  • Targeted weak-area drilling. The setup flow you just came through logged which sections you flagged as weak. Ace Labs surfaces those engines first every time you open it. You are not starting from the beginning — you are starting from where you fell short.
  • Calibrated mocks, not just practice. The cross-tool Survey of Natural Sciences mock (40 Bio + 30 GC + 30 OChem) is timed and DAT-realistic. Wrong answers automatically feed the spaced-rep queue. Every mock you take tells you something specific.
  • No pressure to start at chapter one. Each engine has modular nodes. You can open Gen Chem and go straight to Electrochemistry without sitting through atomic theory. Use the skill tree to skip what you already own.
  • Anxiety-aware design. The interface avoids score countdowns and pass/fail labels during active study. Mocks show performance only after submission, not mid-session. You can break flow at any point; progress saves automatically.

Section D

If you're feeling stuck

Plateau scores are real. So is the feeling that you've seen all this material before and it still isn't clicking. That is a recognizable pattern — and it usually points to one of two things: a gap that wasn't identified correctly, or a study method that isn't forcing active retrieval.

The spaced-rep queue and mock scorecard inside Ace Labs are built to surface those gaps. But if you've been grinding and the number isn't moving, take a step back before pushing harder.

Take a beat before adding more volume.
If a section score is flat across two or more practice attempts, more questions in that section may not be the answer. Look at the error patterns first — are the wrong answers clustered around a specific topic? Are they timing-related? Are they second-guessing errors on things you actually knew?
