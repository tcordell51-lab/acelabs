## 1. Vanilla Method (a.k.a. "Slow Careful Read", "Classic")

- **Origin / popularizer:** Dr. Ari Rezaei, co-founder of DAT Bootcamp. Posted
  on the Bootcamp blog (updated Feb 25, 2026). DAT Booster's Dr. Shahed
  describes the same approach under the label "Classic Approach."
- **Steps (Bootcamp):**
  1. Read the full passage front-to-back, 7–9 min.
  2. Re-read the first paragraph or two at least twice.
  3. Note "special words and names of people, places, and things."
  4. Answer questions in order; allocate 10–12 min total to questions.
  5. Refer back to the text to confirm — "scroll to that particular part
     of the passage where the keyword is."
  6. Stuck? "Simply guess, mark it, and move on."
- **Time-per-passage assumption:** 17–21 min (7–9 read + 10–12 questions).
  Total section budget is 60 min for 3 passages.
- **Strengths:** Deepest comprehension; lowest risk on inference / tone /
  main-idea items; no notation overhead; lowest cognitive friction.
- **Weaknesses:** Tight under real time pressure; useless if you already know
  you don't retain dense text on a single pass.
- **Best-fit student profile:**
  1. Fast / strong readers (Bootcamp implies 250+ WPM via DAT RC Mastery
     "Slow First Pass" gloss).
  2. Native English speakers comfortable with science prose.
  3. Anxious test-takers who panic when "answered a question without knowing
     all the details" (SDN: Charles_Darwin).
- **Quotes:** "what made me feel most comfortable" — Rezaei via Bootcamp blog
  (https://bootcamp.com/blog/vanilla-method-for-dat-reading-comprehension,
  accessed 2026-05-07).

## 2. Search and Destroy (S&D)

- **Origin / popularizer:** Long-standing pre-dental community method; codified
  by Ari Rezaei at DAT Bootcamp and (separately) by Dr. Shahed at DAT Booster.
- **Steps (Bootcamp):**
  1. Skip the passage entirely. Open question 1.
  2. Classify: detail vs. inference / tone / application.
  3. If inference / tone: mark and skip.
  4. If detail: pull a unique keyword (name, place, number, term).
  5. Scan passage paragraph-by-paragraph for the keyword.
  6. Read 1–2 sentences before and after for context, confirm match.
  7. After all detail Qs are done, you've absorbed enough of the passage to
     attempt the marked inference Qs.
- **Time-per-passage assumption:** ~20 min, but front-loaded onto questions
  rather than reading. DAT Booster frames it as "prioritizes question time."
- **Strengths:** Fastest path on detail-heavy passages; "Brutal on retrieval
  questions" (DAT RC Mastery); good safety net if you're slow at sustained
  reading.
- **Weaknesses:** "Catastrophic on inference" (DAT RC Mastery); "may risk
  missing the main idea" (DAT Booster); Rezaei himself reports it "put too
  much pressure on me, and I often ended up doing worse."
- **Best-fit student profile:**
  1. Slow readers / ESL students who time out on full reads.
  2. Students whose practice tests show >70% detail Qs.
  3. Test-anxious students who'd rather "do something" than read passively
     (SDN: HP24 went 17 → 21 after switching to S&D).
- **Sources:**
  https://bootcamp.com/blog/search-destroy-guide-for-dat-reading-comprehension
  (accessed 2026-05-07);
  https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/will-search-and-destroy-method-work-on-dat.1029106/
  (accessed 2026-05-07).

## 3. Map-the-Passage (Passage Mapping / Paragraph Summary)

- **Origin / popularizer:** Multiple — DAT Booster (Dr. Shahed) names it
  "Passage Mapping"; DAT RC Mastery lists it as strategy #1; "Ray" (Bootcamp
  success story, 30 RC) uses a notation variant.
- **Steps (Booster + DAT RC Mastery + Ray):**
  1. Read the passage front-to-back, 9–11 min.
  2. After each paragraph, jot "a brief summary — just a few words — along
     with the paragraph number" (Ray).
  3. Track "section headers, big claims, and contrasting ideas" (Booster).
  4. When you hit questions, use the map to jump to the right paragraph,
     then re-read it locally.
- **Time-per-passage assumption:** 20 min per passage (Ray: 9–11 read + map,
  9–10 questions).
- **Strengths:** Best of any method on tone / main-idea / structural Qs;
  reduces the "I read 600 words and remember nothing" failure mode (DAT RC
  Mastery).
- **Weaknesses:** Slower upfront read; notation discipline required; DAT RC
  Mastery warns it underperforms on pure-mechanism science passages where
  "every paragraph is just more mechanism."
- **Best-fit student profile:**
  1. Steady mid-tier readers (220–280 WPM) who get full passage benefit but
     need a memory aid.
  2. Students strong at structure / argument but weak at recall.
  3. Humanities-leaning students facing science passages — the map externalises
     the structure they can't hold mentally.
- **Sources:** Ray's breakdown at
  https://bootcamp.com/success-stories/rays-detailed-breakdown-for-a-30-in-reading-comprehension;
  https://boosterprep.com/dat/study-guide/top-strategies-to-master-dat-reading-comprehension;
  https://www.datrcmastery.com/blog/dat-reading-comprehension-strategies
  (all accessed 2026-05-07).

## 4. Vicviper Hybrid (Skim-then-S&D)

- **Origin / popularizer:** Pre-dental student "Vicviper," posted at DAT
  Bootcamp; reviewed by Dr. Joel Meyerson. Updated Feb 25, 2026.
- **Steps:**
  1. Review all 17 questions first; pull and memorize candidate keywords —
     names, muscles, chemicals, enzymes, places.
  2. Browse the passage looking for those keywords; when one hits, read
     closely and cross-reference with all questions, not just one.
  3. Build a passing mental map of layout while browsing.
  4. ~3–4 leftover Qs after browse pass; skim again for related terms.
  5. Handle "Which is not mentioned" / inference Qs last.
  6. Self-check the clock — if early passages eat more than 1/3 of section
     time, switch strategy or speed up.
- **Time-per-passage assumption:** Variable — Vicviper notes he exceeded the
  time limit on the real test.
- **Strengths:** Scored a 30 RC (29 on first practice test) using it.
- **Weaknesses:** Author's own caveat: "Very risky because it can take a
  significant amount of time if you get stuck." Requires fast browse speed.
- **Best-fit student profile:**
  1. Fast browsers with strong working memory (hold 17 keyword sets at once).
  2. Science-strong students who recognize technical terms instantly in prose.
  3. Confident test-takers — Vicviper's own disclaimer: "This very well may
     not work for you."
- **Source:** https://bootcamp.com/blog/vicviper-reading-comprehension-strategy
  (accessed 2026-05-07).

## 5. Question Batching / Split-Pass (a.k.a. Staged Reading)

- **Origin / popularizer:** Pre-dental community variant; documented on SDN by
  users smallmediumbig, jttzz112, willbr, and laffytaffy19; DAT RC Mastery
  formalises it as "Question Order Sort" (#8) and "Pre-read the Questions"
  (#10).
- **Steps:**
  1. Pre-read all questions in 60 seconds.
  2. Read first 3–4 paragraphs carefully.
  3. Pause; attempt every question whose anchor is in those paragraphs.
  4. Continue to next paragraph block; repeat.
  5. Save inference / main-idea / tone Qs until after the full passage is
     covered.
- **Time-per-passage assumption:** ~20 min, but smoothed across passage rather
  than front- or back-loaded.
- **Strengths:** Reduces re-reading; ratchets progress so you never "spend 9
  minutes reading and find you can't answer Q1"; resilient to passage-type
  surprise.
- **Weaknesses:** Highest cognitive overhead — three concurrent tasks (read,
  remember question stems, decide whether to commit to an answer now);
  requires practiced rhythm.
- **Best-fit student profile:**
  1. Strong working-memory students.
  2. Mid-tier scorers (18–23) trying to break into 25+ without rebuilding
     reading speed.
  3. Students whose practice diagnostics show a mix of detail and inference
     Qs (not lopsided toward either).
- **Sources:**
  https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/how-to-study-for-dat-reading-comprehension.1294733/;
  https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/dat-rc-strategy.1207324/;
  https://www.datrcmastery.com/blog/dat-reading-comprehension-strategies
  (accessed 2026-05-07).

## 6. Ray's Balanced Method (named variant worth surfacing)

- **Origin:** "Ray," 30 RC, DAT Bootcamp success story.
- **Identity:** Explicit blend of Vanilla + S&D — full read with paragraph
  notation, then keyword-driven re-entry on each question.
- **Time targets:** 9–11 min read + map, 9–10 min questions, full-passage cap
  of 20 min.
- **Quote:** "By balancing time spent on understanding the passage and
  answering questions" — Ray, Bootcamp success story.
- **Fit profile:** Students who can read at a moderate clip and tolerate
  light notation; who lose accuracy with pure S&D but lose time with pure
  Vanilla.
- **Source:** https://bootcamp.com/success-stories/rays-detailed-breakdown-for-a-30-in-reading-comprehension
  (accessed 2026-05-07).

## 7. Other named methods surfaced (lower coverage)

- **Signposting** — Circle transition words (however, therefore, in contrast)
  to track argument shifts. DAT RC Mastery #5. Humanities passages.
- **Chunking** — Break passage into 3–4 sections, summarize after each. DAT
  RC Mastery #4. Universal.
- **Main Idea First** — Read opening + closing sentence of each paragraph
  (~90 sec) before committing to a full strategy. DAT RC Mastery #3.
  Diagnostic / triage move.
- **Skimming (pure)** — 500+ WPM shallow read. DAT RC Mastery #6 warns
  retention drops to ~40%; "Emergency damage control only."
- **BY4YOU** — Mentioned on SDN (Alikhan9) as risky; not characterised in
  current public DAT prep sources.

## Where coaching sources actively contradict each other

1. **S&D as a viable primary method.**
   - **Pro:** Ari Rezaei devotes a full Bootcamp article to S&D; DAT Booster
     lists it as a top-4 strategy; SDN HP24 reports 17 → 21.
   - **Con:** Rezaei *himself* admits S&D "put too much pressure on me,
     and I often ended up doing worse" — i.e., the same author who teaches
     it doesn't use it. The 30-RC scorer at
     https://forums.studentdoctor.net/threads/dat-breakdown-24-aa-30-rc.1499487/
     explicitly rejects "search-and-destroy methods" because they "led me to
     lose focus."

2. **Whether to write notes during the read.**
   - **Pro-notes:** Ray (30 RC), Booster's Passage Mapping, DAT RC Mastery
     Chunking + Paragraph Summary.
   - **Anti-notes:** Vanilla method takes none. Vicviper (30 RC) takes none —
     uses keyword recall instead.

3. **One-method-fits-all vs. passage-type switching.**
   - **One method:** SDN dentalschool123: "Pick a strategy and stick with
     it."
   - **Switch per passage:** DAT RC Mastery argues the wrong tool on the
     wrong passage drops accuracy "by 10 to 15 points"; explicitly tells
     students to diagnose passage type first (Main Idea First, #3) before
     committing.

4. **Question batching vs. linear order.**
   - **Linear:** Vanilla and S&D both work question 1 → 17.
   - **Batched:** DAT RC Mastery #8 ("Question Order Sort") and SDN's
     willbr / smallmediumbig advocate answering easy first.

5. **Whether speed-reading helps.**
   - **Pro:** DAT RC Mastery's "Slow First Pass" assumes 250+ WPM as
     prerequisite; Bootcamp's Vanilla allots 7–9 min for a ~600-word passage
     which implies ~120 WPM but expects re-reads.
   - **Con:** DAT RC Mastery #6 actively discourages skimming above 500 WPM
     ("retain about 40%"); Orgoman / DAT Destroyer recommend slowing down
     to "pause and restate the sentence in simpler terms."
