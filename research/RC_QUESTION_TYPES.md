# DAT Reading Comprehension — Question-Type Taxonomy & Per-Type Strategy

Research compiled 2026-05-07. All URLs accessed 2026-05-07 via WebSearch + WebFetch. No fabricated citations; where a number is not in a source, the file says so.

---

## 1. Section structure (anchor for everything below)

- 3 passages, ~1,100–1,500 words each, ~14 paragraphs total across the section.
- 50 items total, ~16–17 questions per passage.
- 60 minutes; ~20 min/passage budget; ~72 sec/question raw average.
- Splits commonly cited: 6–8 min read / 12 min questions (Kaplan, Booster), or 9–11 min read+map / 9–10 min questions (Ray, 30-scorer on DAT Bootcamp).

Sources: Kaplan ("What's Tested on the DAT: RC"), DAT Booster ("Top Strategies"), DAT Bootcamp ("Ray's Detailed Breakdown for a 30"), datprep.com, ADA DAT User Manual.

## 2. Canonical question types (consensus across 2023–2026 prep sources)

No DAT prep source publishes exact official percentages — ADA does not release a question-type blueprint for RC. The frequency column below is the **consensus relative weight** across DAT Bootcamp, DAT Booster, Kaplan, datprep.com, Shemmassian, and CrackDAT. Where a source quantifies, it is cited; otherwise the cell says "consensus: most common / common / less common / rare."

| # | Type | Frequency (consensus) | Core strategy | Time budget | Signature trap |
|---|------|----------------------|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Detail (straight retrieval)** | Most common — "by far the most common" (DAT Bootcamp S&D guide; datprep.com; Kaplan) | Search & Destroy: pull a unique keyword (name, enzyme, year, place) from the stem and scan for it in the passage; verify the surrounding 1–2 sentences match the stem. | 30–50 sec | **Decoy** — DAT plants the same keyword in multiple places; the matching sentence describes a different sub-fact (DAT Bootcamp, "Common Traps") |
| 2 | **Detail EXCEPT / NOT / "least"** | Common — at least 1–2 per passage in most takers' reports | Treat as four mini-detail questions: T/F each choice against the passage. Don't trust memory; physically check three of four. | 60–90 sec (longest detail type) | **Half-right / half-wrong** — choice contains one true clause and one false clause; students stop at the true half (DAT Bootcamp, "Common Traps") |
| 3 | **Main Idea / Global / Best Title / Primary Purpose** | Less common — often 1, sometimes 2 per passage (Kaplan: "Global"); Booster lists as one of four core types | Map-based: derive from a 2–4 word per-paragraph map (Vicviper/Ray method) rather than rereading. Eliminate choices that describe one paragraph only. | 45–75 sec — fast if you mapped; slow if you didn't | **Out-of-scope / one-paragraph trap** — a choice that's true but only covers ¶2; LSAT calls the same pattern "describes a specific part rather than the majority" (Kaplan LSAT RC) |
| 4 | **Inference / Conclusion** | Common — Booster, Kaplan, datprep.com all list as a top-4 category | Anchor to a specific sentence; the right answer is one logical step from text, never two. Watch transitions: *however, despite, although, yet* often house the inferable shift (datprep.com). | 60–90 sec | **Extreme language / unsupported leap** — choices with *always, never, all, must, proves*; right answer usually hedges (*may, suggests, is consistent with*) |
| 5 | **Tone / Author Attitude / Bias** | Less common — ~0–2 per passage; Kaplan and CrackDAT list as own category | Find evaluative adjectives & adverbs (*promising, controversial, regrettable, surprisingly*). Tone is almost never "indifferent" or "hostile" on DAT — it's usually a calibrated *cautiously optimistic / neutral-informative / mildly skeptical*. | 45–60 sec | **Extreme tone words** — *contemptuous, euphoric, dismissive* — almost always wrong on DAT (Kaplan; Booster) |
| 6 | **Function / Structure (why is this here?)** | Less common — Kaplan and Shemmassian list explicitly | Re-read the cited line **plus** the sentence before and after. Function = role in the argument (example, counterpoint, definition, transition, evidence). | 45–75 sec | **Decoy** — answer that restates *what* the sentence says rather than *why* it's there |
| 7 | **Vocabulary / Definition in Context** | Rare on DAT (much smaller than SAT) — datprep.com and Jack Westin call out "terminology" sub-type; Kaplan does not list it separately | Find the term; substitute each answer into the sentence; pick the one that preserves meaning. The DAT defines most jargon in-passage. | 30–45 sec | **Dictionary trap** — primary dictionary meaning that doesn't fit the passage's specialized usage |
| 8 | **Application / Strengthen–Weaken / Hypothetical** | Rare on DAT — Kaplan lists "Strengthen/Weaken"; most breakdowns say 0–2 per section, some forms see zero | Identify the author's claim, then ask which choice adds evidence for/against. Do not bring outside science knowledge. | 75–120 sec (highest variance) | **Outside knowledge** — choice that's scientifically true but unsupported by the passage |
| 9 | **Causal / Sequence** | Embedded inside Detail/Inference; datprep.com and Jack Westin call out as sub-type | Sketch arrow chains (A → B → C) on scratch paper for any passage with mechanism or process. | 45–75 sec | **Reversal** — cause and effect flipped, or sequence reordered (DAT Bootcamp, "Common Traps") |

**Frequency caveat repeated:** ADA does not publish a question-type blueprint. The "most/common/less/rare" labels are the consensus across DAT Bootcamp, DAT Booster, Kaplan, Shemmassian, datprep.com, CrackDAT, and forum breakdowns. Treat as rank order, not percentages.

## 3. Per-type strategy detail (what each major prep teaches)

### DAT Bootcamp
- **Vanilla method (Dr. Ari Rezaei).** Read 7–9 min, answer in order 10–12 min. Pre-read ¶1–2 twice for orientation. No skipping by question type — order-of-test. ([bootcamp.com/blog/vanilla-method-for-dat-reading-comprehension](https://bootcamp.com/blog/vanilla-method-for-dat-reading-comprehension), accessed 2026-05-07)
- **Search & Destroy.** Skip the passage; go to questions. Answer detail-based; **skip & flag application, inference, tone** for a second pass. Rationale: by the time you've answered the detail questions you've effectively read the passage in fragments. ([bootcamp.com/blog/search-destroy-guide-for-dat-reading-comprehension](https://bootcamp.com/blog/search-destroy-guide-for-dat-reading-comprehension), accessed 2026-05-07)
- **Vicviper.** Read all 16–17 questions first, list keywords, then read the passage one time, hunting keywords. Save for last: "which is **not** mentioned," T/F-reasoning, and questions that resist keyword search. ([bootcamp.com/blog/vicviper-reading-comprehension-strategy](https://bootcamp.com/blog/vicviper-reading-comprehension-strategy), accessed 2026-05-07)
- **Ray (30 scorer).** Hybrid map + S&D. 9–11 min read with paragraph-numbered keyword bullets; 9–10 min questions. Mark + guess + move on for anything > ~75 sec. ([bootcamp.com/success-stories/rays-detailed-breakdown-for-a-30-in-reading-comprehension](https://bootcamp.com/success-stories/rays-detailed-breakdown-for-a-30-in-reading-comprehension), accessed 2026-05-07)

### DAT Booster
- Four core types: Main Idea, Detail, Inference, Tone.
- Four strategies, matched to passage type: **Classic** (fast readers), **Search & Destroy** (detail-heavy passages), **Passage Mapping** (tone/inference-heavy passages), **Hybrid**. Booster explicitly says **Mapping is the right strategy when tone and inference questions dominate**. ([boosterprep.com/dat/study-guide/top-strategies-to-master-dat-reading-comprehension](https://boosterprep.com/dat/study-guide/top-strategies-to-master-dat-reading-comprehension), accessed 2026-05-07)

### Kaplan
- Seven labels: **Global, Detail, Detail EXCEPT, Tone, Function, Inference, Strengthen/Weaken**. ([kaptest.com/study/dat/whats-tested-on-the-dat-reading-comprehension](https://www.kaptest.com/study/dat/whats-tested-on-the-dat-reading-comprehension/), accessed 2026-05-07 — fetched via search snippet; direct fetch returned 403)
- Time targets: 20 min/passage, 40–45 sec/question average, with longer items absorbing the slack from shorter detail items.

### datprep.com
- Lists Detail (most common), Tone, Inference, Main Idea/Title. Inference questions cluster at transition words (*despite, however, but, yet, although*). ([datprep.com/breakdown-and-question-pattern-of-dat-reading-comprehension-test](https://datprep.com/breakdown-and-question-pattern-of-dat-reading-comprehension-test/), accessed 2026-05-07)

### Shemmassian / CrackDAT (Medium)
- Five types: Overall Comprehension, Detail, Tone, Author's Purpose, Function. CrackDAT also explicitly tags Causal Relationships and Definition/Terminology as sub-types. ([dentaladmissiontest.medium.com/your-guide-to-the-dats-reading-comprehension-rc-section-ca07d15c159a](https://dentaladmissiontest.medium.com/your-guide-to-the-dats-reading-comprehension-rc-section-ca07d15c159a), accessed 2026-05-07)

### Mike Christiansen / "Mike's Videos"
- Confirmed via DAT Bootcamp and forum sources that Mike Christiansen is a **DAT chemistry** instructor — his DAT content is GenChem/OChem-focused, **not RC**. No published RC method under his name. ([bootcamp.com/expert-educators/dr-mike-christiansen](https://bootcamp.com/expert-educators/dr-mike-christiansen), accessed 2026-05-07). Treat any "Mike on RC" reference as misattribution.

### Chad's, Magoosh
- **Chad's Prep / Chad Vogt** content is chemistry/math-focused; no dedicated DAT RC method found in 2023–2026 sources.
- **Magoosh** has DAT prep, but its RC content is not surfaced separately in 2023–2026 results; its LSAT RC framework (Main Point, Inference) does parallel the DAT taxonomy used above. ([magoosh.com/lsat/lsat-reading-comprehension-question-types](https://magoosh.com/lsat/lsat-reading-comprehension-question-types/), accessed 2026-05-07)

## 4. Named traps (cross-referenced)

DAT Bootcamp's "Common Traps on the DAT Reading Comprehension Section" names **three** explicitly: **Decoys, Reversals, Half Right / Half Wrong**. ([bootcamp.com/blog/common-traps-on-the-dat-reading-comprehension-section](https://bootcamp.com/blog/common-traps-on-the-dat-reading-comprehension-section/), accessed 2026-05-07)

Adding patterns from Kaplan/Booster/forum breakdowns:

| Trap | Mechanic | Hits hardest on |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| **Decoy** | Same keyword appears in 2+ paragraphs; one sentence is the real referent, the others are bait | Detail, Function |
| **Reversal** | Cause/effect or sequence flipped | Causal, Inference, EXCEPT |
| **Half right / half wrong** | One clause true, one false | EXCEPT, Detail, Main Idea |
| **Extreme language** | *always, never, all, must, only, proves* | Inference, Tone |
| **Out-of-scope (one-paragraph)** | True for ¶2 only, but stem asks the whole passage | Main Idea, Primary Purpose |
| **Outside-knowledge** | Choice is true in real chemistry/biology but unsupported by the passage | Application, Strengthen/Weaken, Inference |
| **Dictionary** | Primary dictionary meaning rather than in-context meaning | Vocabulary |
| **Extreme tone** | *contemptuous, hostile, euphoric, dismissive* — almost always wrong | Tone |
| **Familiar-words paraphrase** | Choice reuses passage phrases but rewords to no longer answer the question | Detail, Function, Inference |

## 5. Per-type time budget — what top scorers report

Ray (30 RC, DAT Bootcamp): 20 min/passage flat; mark-and-move at ~75 sec.
Ari/Vanilla (DAT Bootcamp): 7–9 min read, 10–12 min answer.
Kaplan: 8 min read / 12 min answer, 40–45 sec/q average.
Booster: 6–8 min read / 10–12 min answer.

Synthesized per-type targets (consensus, not officially published):
- Pure detail with a unique keyword: **30–50 sec**.
- Detail EXCEPT / NOT: **60–90 sec** (four checks).
- Inference: **60–90 sec**.
- Tone: **45–60 sec**.
- Function: **45–75 sec**.
- Main Idea: **45–75 sec**, fast if mapped.
- Application / Strengthen-Weaken: **75–120 sec** — highest variance.
- Vocabulary in context: **30–45 sec**.

Hard discipline shared by every 28+ scorer: **mark + guess + move at the per-question cap; never exceed 2× the average on a single question.**

## 6. S&D vs Mapping — when each wins, and whether to switch mid-passage

| Mix during passage | Best strategy |
|--------------------|---------------|
| Stem reads as keyword-rich (names, dates, enzymes, locations); ≥10 of 16 questions are detail/EXCEPT | **Search & Destroy** — don't waste read time |
| Tone, function, primary-purpose, inference together exceed ~5 questions, or paragraphs use heavy contrast/argument structure | **Mapping** (Vicviper / Ray / Booster Mapping) — you need passage architecture |
| You can't tell after scanning 6–8 stems | **Hybrid**: 60-sec skim of first sentence of each paragraph → 2–4 word map → S&D the detail questions, then return to inference/tone with the map in hand |

**Should strategy shift mid-passage?** Yes — but only one direction, S&D → Mapping. If you started S&D and discover the questions are mostly inference/tone, stop, read the passage once with a fast paragraph map, then return. The reverse (mapped, then abandoning the map) wastes the read; finish in S&D mode using the map you already built.

Sources: DAT Bootcamp S&D and Vicviper guides; DAT Booster "Top Strategies"; DAT RC Mastery "10 RC strategies, ranked by passage type" ([datrcmastery.com/blog/dat-reading-comprehension-strategies](https://www.datrcmastery.com/blog/dat-reading-comprehension-strategies), accessed 2026-05-07).

## 7. Cross-test comparison — DAT RC vs MCAT CARS vs LSAT RC vs SAT R&W

| Dimension | DAT RC | MCAT CARS | LSAT RC | SAT R&W (digital) |
|-----------|--------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| Source material | Science, mostly biology/medicine, occasionally history-of-science | Humanities + social sciences only — no natural science | Law, humanities, social science, science | Short cross-domain passages, often literature |
| Question category mix | **Detail-heavy.** Inference, tone, main idea minority | Detail / inference / tone collapsed into three AAMC skills: Foundations of Comprehension (~30%), Reasoning Within the Text (~30%), Reasoning Beyond the Text (~40%) ([AAMC](https://students-residents.aamc.org/critical-analysis-and-reasoning-skills/critical-analysis-and-reasoning-skills-section-skill-3-reasoning-beyond-text-passage-types), accessed 2026-05-07) | Main Point + Inference + Function dominate; "primary purpose" appears nearly every passage | Main Idea, Detail, Inference, Vocab-in-context, Function, Cross-text |
| Application / "beyond the text" | **Rare** (0–2 items / section) | **Heavy** — the defining CARS category | Light | Absent |
| Outside knowledge allowed? | No (but rare violations) | **No — strictly forbidden**; AAMC tests this explicitly | No | No |
| Per-question time | ~72 sec | ~100 sec | ~85 sec | ~70 sec |
| Best parallel for DAT students | — | Worst transfer; CARS overweights what DAT underweights | Inference and Main Point overlap well | Detail and Vocab transfer cleanly |

**DAT-specific signature.** Passages are scientific but ADA does not test scientific reasoning — only retrieval and light inference. The DAT is essentially **LSAT-detail-flavor on MCAT-topic content, minus CARS-style application.** Coaching that imports CARS "reasoning beyond text" drills onto DAT students misallocates time; the DAT's bottleneck is fast detail retrieval under fatigue, not abstract reasoning.

Sources for comparison: AAMC CARS Skills 1–3 pages; Kaplan LSAT RC pages; College Board "Reading and Writing — SAT Suite"; PrepScholar SAT R&W breakdown; UWorld MCAT CARS overview. All accessed 2026-05-07.
