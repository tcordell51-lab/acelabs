Assembly workflow — turn your scenes into a real TikTok

  1. Pick a mode. Audio-only if you'll voiceover the existing animated TikTok template. Audio+face if you want picture-in-picture for native TikTok feel.
  2. Record each scene. Read the script aloud at the pace you'd say it during a lesson. Re-record any that don't feel right.
  3. Download all. Files arrive named <tiktok>-<scene>-<phase>-<mode>.webm.
  4. Drop into your editor. CapCut Web (free), DaVinci Resolve, or Final Cut. Import the animated TikTok HTML as a screen recording (or open it and screen-capture full-screen).
  5. Layer the audio over the screen recording — each audio scene aligns to its t= timestamp in the original timeline.
  6. (Face mode) Drop the face videos as a circle picture-in-picture (CapCut: "Mask" → Circle). Position top-right or bottom-left. Standard TikTok face-cam size: ~25% of screen height.
  7. Add music (royalty-free or TikTok library). Trending sounds boost reach but use lower volume so voice cuts through.
  8. Trim aggressively. If your final cut is over 45 sec, the algorithm typically penalizes. Cut intro silence, tighten beats.
  9. Export 1080×1920 MP4 and post.

Pro tip: Record face scenes against a clean wall in good light. The voice quality matters more than the cinematography — students will forgive a plain background but not a muddy mic.
