Test-day mechanics

What real DAT day
actually feels like.

Five minutes. The interface quirks, timing rules, and physical-center details that catch even well-prepared students off guard — because no prep tool puts them in writing.

01 of 08  ·  Interface

The navigation lag is real.

Students report a roughly half-second delay between pressing "Next" and the next question appearing on the Prometric interface. Every click. Every question.

This is not your connection or the computer. The Prometric DAT delivery software has a measurable response lag that shows up between questions, between passages, and when the review screen loads. If you have never practiced with it, your first instinct on test day will be to click again — and double-clicking can skip a question or register an unintended answer.

What to do: Click once, then hold. Resist the urge to click again during the pause. Practice this on every Prometric-format mock you run before test day so the rhythm is automatic.

02 of 08  ·  Interface

Marking is not the same as free skipping.

The DAT interface has a "Mark" button, but you cannot freely navigate to any question at will. You can mark a question to flag it, then use the End-of-Section review screen to return to marked items only.

Students sometimes expect a sidebar list where they can jump to any question mid-section, as some third-party mocks offer. That is not how the Prometric interface works. On the real exam, your forward movement through questions is linear. You can mark a question as you go, and at the end of the section you get a review screen that shows all questions with their status — answered, unanswered, marked. From there you can revisit any marked or unanswered item. There is no mid-section free navigation.

Demo — mark behavior
Mark a question, then continue forward. You return to it at section review.

What to do: Build a clear rule for yourself now. For example: if a question will take more than 90 seconds, mark it and move on. Budget 10-15 seconds at end of section to hit the review screen and address marked items. Practice this exact workflow in your next full mock.

03 of 08  ·  Interface

RC passages reset to the top on each question.

Students have reported that when you advance to a new question in Reading Comprehension, the passage panel scrolls back to the top — even if your previous question required reading something near the bottom.

On the real exam, RC questions appear alongside a scrollable passage. When you move to the next question in the set, the scroll position resets. If consecutive questions both reference content in paragraph 4 or 5, you will re-scroll each time. Students who do not expect this waste time assuming the passage "loaded wrong" and are surprised to find themselves scrolling repeatedly.

What to do: During practice, track approximately which paragraph contains key evidence for each question type. On test day, scroll directly to that region without hunting. For main-idea or tone questions that require no specific quote, do not scroll at all — answer from your read-through.

04 of 08  ·  Timing

Your break timer starts the moment the break begins.

There is no grace period. Your optional break timer begins immediately when your scheduled break period opens. If you spend two minutes in the check-out / check-in protocol, those two minutes come out of your break.

The DAT has an optional break between the Biology section and the Survey of Natural Sciences. The break is timed. The check-out process — raising your hand, waiting for the proctor, signing out, walking to the waiting area — takes real time. The same is true coming back: you sign in, get reseated, go through the check-in process, and only then resume. Students who have not planned for this are sometimes surprised to find 4-5 minutes gone before they reach the bathroom.

What to do: Plan your break activities in order of priority. If you need the bathroom, that is first. Water and a snack should be waiting at your assigned locker. Know exactly what you are going to do and do it in that order, without hesitation. Do not browse your phone looking for test tips during the break.

05 of 08  ·  Tools

There is a calculator in QR. Use it.

The Prometric interface provides an on-screen calculator for the Quantitative Reasoning section. Multiple students have reported not knowing it existed until they were already deep into the section.

The calculator button appears in the toolbar. It is a basic four-function calculator with some additional operations. It does not do symbolic algebra, does not graph, and does not factor — but for arithmetic-heavy steps in rate, ratio, and geometry problems, it can meaningfully reduce errors. Students who discover it mid-section and have never practiced using an on-screen calculator in exam conditions tend to use it poorly or not at all.

06 of 08  ·  Tools

Keyboard shortcuts exist in SNS and PAT.

Students who knew about keyboard shortcuts in the Survey of Natural Sciences and Perceptual Ability sections saved time on navigation. Students who did not know spent those same moments reaching for the mouse.

The Prometric delivery software supports keyboard navigation for answer selection and moving between questions in several sections. In SNS, answer choices can typically be selected with the corresponding letter key. In PAT, similar keyboard actions apply. The exact key mappings students have reported are listed below. Because the interface is not publicly documented in detail, treat these as "reported behavior" and verify in any full-length Prometric-format mock you run.

What to do: Run at least one full Prometric-format mock with the keyboard next to your mouse. Test each shortcut early in the section when the stakes of an error are low. If a shortcut behaves differently than listed above, note it — then keep using whatever is faster for you.

07 of 08  ·  Physical center

Prometric centers have rules that affect your comfort.

Mask requirements, food and water restrictions, ID rules, and locker policies vary by center and have changed over time. Do not assume your experience at another testing center (MCAT, GRE, etc.) will match.

A few specific points students have flagged after test day:

  • Glasses + mask fogging. If you wear glasses and your center requires a mask, the combination can cause lens fogging that is genuinely distracting. Students have reported spending 30-60 seconds between sections adjusting their mask or glasses. Bring a mask with a nose wire and practice wearing it while studying in the weeks before your test.
  • Food and water. You cannot bring food or drinks into the testing room. Items go in a locker. You can access your locker only during a scheduled break. Plan accordingly — eat before arrival, and leave a small snack and water in the locker for the break.
  • ID requirements. You need a current, government-issued photo ID. Expired IDs are typically not accepted. Confirm the specific accepted ID list on the ADA (American Dental Association) and Prometric websites before test day, since policies update.
  • Personal items. Phones, smartwatches, and most personal items must be stored. Many centers require you to empty your pockets completely. Wear comfortable clothing without pockets that could trigger any concern.
  • Earplugs. Many centers offer foam earplugs. Ask when you arrive. These are often not advertised, but students report they are available if you ask the proctor.

What to do: Call your specific Prometric center 2-3 days before your test date and confirm their current mask policy, ID requirements, and locker access procedure. A 5-minute phone call eliminates 30 minutes of test-morning anxiety.

08 of 08  ·  Your rights

You are allowed to ask the proctor.

Many students assume they must silently tolerate any problem — a flickering monitor, loud noise from an adjacent station, or an urgent need for the restroom mid-section. You do not have to.

Proctors are there to administer the exam and to help resolve issues that could compromise your performance. Several situations where raising your hand is appropriate and will not penalize you:

  • Technical problem at your station. If your screen freezes, your mouse stops responding, or you notice something wrong with the software display, raise your hand immediately. The proctor can note the issue and, if necessary, contact Prometric support. Do not wait to see if it resolves itself.
  • Restroom during a section. You can request a restroom break mid-section. The section timer will continue running. The proctor will escort you out and back in. This is not ideal, but if you genuinely need it, do not wait through 20 questions in discomfort.
  • Disruptive noise or environment. If another test-taker is causing significant noise, you can raise your hand and ask the proctor to address it. They may offer earplugs or move the other person.
  • Scratch paper or whiteboard replacement. You will be given scratch materials (the form varies by center — laminated boards are common). If you run out of space, raise your hand and ask for more.

What to do: Know before you arrive that raising your hand is a tool, not a disruption. You have already prepared for the content. Do not let a solvable environmental problem knock you off course.
