Section 1 Organic Chemistry 26 cards
Pattern Shortcuts (Thomas Method)
Pattern #1
LPOP high yield
Lone Pair on ring = ortho/para director

Any substituent with a lone pair on its ring-attached atom pumps electron density into the ortho and para positions via resonance. The incoming electrophile lands where electrons are richest.

When to useEAS question with -NH2, -OH, -OR, or any substituent bearing a lone pair on the atom bonded to the ring. Halogens qualify too (see Pattern #3).
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #2
Flip the W = Meta
Withdrawing groups direct meta

Electron-withdrawing groups (-NO2, -C=O, -COOH, -CN, -CF3) drain ortho/para, leaving meta as the only electron-rich spot. Memory hook: physically flip the letter "W" upside-down — it becomes "M."

When to useAny EAS with a carbonyl, nitro, or other pi-withdrawing substituent already on the ring.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #3
Halogens: the Exception DAT trap
Deactivating AND ortho/para

Halogens withdraw through the sigma bond (ring reacts slower) but donate lone pairs through pi (electrons go to o/p). Net: deactivator that still directs ortho/para. The only split class.

When to useAny ring with F, Cl, Br, or I already attached. Slower reaction than benzene, but still o/p selectivity.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #4
Magic Numbers: 2, 6, 10
(4n+2) pi electrons = aromatic

A ring is aromatic when it is flat, fully conjugated, every atom contributes a p-orbital, AND has 2, 6, or 10 pi electrons (Huckel: 4n+2). 4 pi electrons = antiaromatic (avoid). Furan counts the O lone pair as 2 pi electrons.

When to useIdentifying aromatic vs. antiaromatic vs. non-aromatic. Predicting stability of cations/anions in rings.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #5
Aromaticity Trumps Resonance
Aromatic stability beats any other resonance

When aromaticity and plain resonance conflict on which structure is most stable, aromaticity wins every time. Reactions that form an aromatic ring are strongly favored; those that break one are strongly opposed.

When to useDeciding dominant resonance structure, or predicting whether a reaction will proceed spontaneously.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #6
CARDIO high yield
Charge · Atom · Resonance · Dipole · Induction · Orbital

Acid strength = conjugate base stability. Run CARDIO top-to-bottom: (C) neutral base > anionic base; (A) bigger atom spreads charge (HI > HBr > HCl > HF); (R) resonance-stabilized anion = stronger acid; (D/I) nearby EN atoms stabilize; (O) sp > sp2 > sp3 orbital stability. First factor that differs decides.

When to useAny "rank these acids/bases" question. Also predicts which proton gets removed.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #7
"C" in Catalyst = Cis
Lindlar = Cis · Na/NH3 = Trans

Lindlar's catalyst (Pd/CaCO3/Pb/quinoline) reduces alkynes to cis-alkenes. Hook: the C in "Catalyst" stands for Cis. Na/NH3(l) dissolving-metal reduction gives trans. These two are the most-tested alkyne pair.

When to useAny alkyne reduction question. Identify the reagent, apply the hook, write the stereochemistry.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #8
Palindromic N2H4 = Wolff-Kishner
N2H4 reads the same both ways

Hydrazine (N2H4) is a palindrome. N2H4 / KOH / heat = Wolff-Kishner: C=O becomes CH2. Clemmensen (Zn(Hg)/HCl) gives the same result under acidic conditions. Both fully deoxygenate a ketone or aldehyde.

When to useAny problem converting a carbonyl to a methylene. Choose Wolff-Kishner for base-sensitive substrates, Clemmensen for acid-tolerant ones.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #9
Oxidation = Bonds to O
Count bonds to oxygen

Skip the half-reaction math. In organic: oxidation = adding bonds to oxygen. Alcohol (1 bond) → aldehyde/ketone (2 bonds) → acid/ester/amide (3 bonds). Each step up = oxidation; each step down = reduction.

When to useAny carbonyl ladder question. Immediately tells you whether you need an oxidant or reductant and how many steps to travel.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #10
PCC on 2° OH = Ketone DAT trap
PCC doesn't always stop at aldehyde

PCC stops 1° alcohols at the aldehyde (not COOH like Jones). But on a 2° alcohol, PCC gives a ketone — not an aldehyde. 3° alcohols: no reaction. The most-missed PCC question on the DAT.

When to useAny PCC question. Check the substrate class first. 1° → aldehyde. 2° → ketone. 3° → no reaction.
Open in OChem engine
Pattern #11
Delta (heat) in SN-vs-E = Elimination
See heat with Nu/base? Think alkene

When the question is SN vs. E, the heat symbol tips the balance toward elimination. Elimination makes more particles (higher entropy), so heat favors it. Caveat: heat appears in many other reactions, so read context first.

When to useAlkyl halide + base + heat, or alcohol + H+ + heat. Heat flag usually seals it for E1/E2.

Core Principles
Principle #1
Frameworks > Lists
Understand WHY, not WHAT

Don't memorize 30 strong acids. Understand why they're strong (apply CARDIO). One framework compresses thousands of facts into a handful of rules that work on any molecule, known or unknown.

When to useEvery time you catch yourself memorizing a list, step back and ask "what's the underlying framework?"

Principle #2
Pattern Recognition Beats Memorization
Hooks become reflexes under time pressure

Your brain is a pattern-matcher, not a database. Palindromic hooks, visual flips, silly mnemonics — these bypass conscious memory and become automatic. Drill until they fire in under 0.5 seconds.

When to useTest-day speed. Seconds saved per question add up when you have 30 questions in 30 minutes.

Principle #3
Flag Counter-Intuitive Traps Early
Memorize the exceptions

Some things are just weird: CH3 is "bulkier" than OH in CIP priority. Halogens are EWGs but o/p directors. These feel wrong but they're the traps the DAT loves. Flag them early.

When to useAny problem where your gut says "obvious" but the rules look unusual. Pause and check if this is a known trap.

Chapter Summaries
Chapter 1
Stereochemistry
Verify 4 different groups before R/S

A carbon is chiral only if it has 4 different groups — check this first. Then assign CIP priorities (atomic number, then next atom out). Put lowest in the back. Trace 1→2→3: clockwise = R, counter-clockwise = S. If the lowest is in front, flip your answer.

When to useR/S assignments, meso compound checks, enantiomer vs. diastereomer questions.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 2
Acidity & Basicity (CARDIO)
Acid strength = conjugate base stability

Run CARDIO (Charge → Atom → Resonance → Dipole → Induction → Orbital). First factor that differs between two acids decides the winner. Basicity is the reverse: most basic = least stable as an anion.

When to useEvery "rank these" acid/base question, and when predicting which proton gets removed in complex molecules.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 3
Aromaticity & EAS
LPOP + Flip-the-W + Halogen exception

Aromatic rings accept E+ only by kicking out H+ (arenium mechanism). Substituents control where: LPOP (lone pair → o/p), Flip-the-W (EWG → meta), Halogens (deactivating but o/p).

When to useEAS halogenation, nitration, sulfonation, Friedel-Crafts, directing effect questions.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 4
SN1 / SN2 / E1 / E2
4-factor decision tree

Four filters: substrate class (1°/2°/3°), nucleophile/base strength, solvent (polar aprotic → SN2; polar protic → SN1/E1), temperature (heat → E). Strong base → E. Good Nu, not base → SN. Bulky base → Hofmann. No base + heat + protic → SN1/E1.

When to useAny alkyl halide + Nu/base question. Apply all 4 filters before writing the product.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 5
Addition & Markovnikov
Trust the intermediate — it tells you everything

Free cation intermediate: Markov regio + rearranges + racemic. Bridged 3-ring (halonium/mercurinium): anti stereo + no rearrange. Concerted (BH3, OsO4): syn + no rearrange. Identify intermediate before writing product.

When to useAll alkene additions: HX, H2O, X2, halohydrin, Hg, BH3, mCPBA, H2, OsO4, ozonolysis.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 6
Alkene/Alkyne Reactions
More substituted = more stable

Universal rule: more substituents = more stable. True for cations (3° > 2° > 1°), alkenes (Zaitsev), radicals (benzylic > 3° > 2° > 1°), enolates. When in doubt, pick the product with more non-H groups on the key carbon.

When to use"Major product" or "most stable" questions in alkene/alkyne transformations.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 7
Carbonyl Ox/Red Hierarchy
Bonds to O = oxidation level

Ladder: 0 (alkane) → 1 (alcohol) → 2 (aldehyde/ketone) → 3 (acid/ester/amide). Up = needs oxidant (PCC, Jones, KMnO4). Down = needs reductant (NaBH4, LiAlH4, DIBAL-H). Count bonds to pick reagent.

When to useAny "convert A to B" question on the carbonyl ladder.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 8
Enolates, Grignard, Michael
All about the alpha-carbon

The alpha-carbon (adjacent to C=O, pKa ~20) loses H+ to form an enolate (carbanion attacks other C=O). Grignard is a carbanion attacking C=O directly. Michael = enolate + alpha,beta-unsaturated carbonyl (1,4-addition). All three funnel nucleophiles into C-C bond formation.

When to useAldol, Claisen, Grignard, Michael, and any C-C bond-forming question at a carbonyl.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 9
Spectroscopy (IR + NMR)
4-step strategy: IR first, then NMR
IR: C=O ~1700 | broad OH ~3200-3600 | N-H ~3300-3500 | C=N/C=C ~2200 | C-H ~2900-3100

NMR: (1) count peaks = # of H environments. (2) integration = H ratio. (3) splitting = n+1 neighbors. (4) shift = chemical environment. Match the pattern, confirm with IR.

When to useEvery spec question. Run the 4-step every time without skipping IR.
Open in OChem engine
Chapter 10
Test-Taking Moves
Answer choices tell you what's tested

Read the answer choices first. They reveal what's being tested. If all four options have "syn," stereo isn't the test — regio is. Eliminate 2 fast, think hard on the remaining two. Don't second-guess clean pattern matches.

When to useEvery question under time pressure. Also for verifying your reasoning before committing.

Section 2 Quantitative Reasoning 6 cards
Sarah's Tricks
QR Trick #1
The Baseball Trick high yield
log base b of x = y → everyone moves up a spot

Picture a baseball field. The base of the log (small number) warms up in the box. The argument is at home plate. The result is at first base. To convert to exponent form, everyone moves up one spot: the base becomes the exponent base, the argument crosses the equals sign, the result becomes the exponent.

log2(8) = 3 → 23 = 8
When to useAny standalone log equation. See a log by itself — do the baseball thing. Everyone moves up one spot.
Open in QR engine
QR Trick #2
The Dice Pyramid
2-dice sums — no math, just the pyramid

Write the pyramid every time — takes 5 seconds. Sums 2 through 12, counts underneath: 1·2·3·4·5·6·5·4·3·2·1. The peak is 6 ways at sum 7. Probability = count / 36. Sum = 9? Look up 9 → 4 ways → 4/36 = 1/9. Compound: P(6 or 9) → 5+4 = 9/36. Only works for sums — not differences or "both even."

Pyramid: 1·2·3·4·5·6·5·4·3·2·1 over 36
When to useAny 2-dice sum probability. "Brain break" question — zero calculation needed.
Open in QR engine
QR Trick #3
The C-Method
Combinations without the formula

Draw a literal C. The larger number goes on the left (top of the C), the smaller on the right. On top: expand the larger counting down by the smaller (for C(10,3): write 10×9×8). On bottom: the smaller as a full factorial (3×2×1). Divide. Self-check: same number of digits on top and bottom.

C(10,3): top = 10×9×8 / bottom = 3×2×1 = 120
When to useAny combination (committees, card hands, unordered groups). Order doesn't matter? Draw the C.
Open in QR engine
QR Trick #4
Shield + Tornado
Absolute value: inside is protected, outside flips

The absolute-value bars are shields — what's inside doesn't change. A tornado rips through and flips everything outside: signs flip, inequality direction flips. There are always two answers: easy (drop bars, solve) and hard/tornado (flip everything outside, solve again). Isolate the absolute value first, then apply.

When to useAny absolute-value equation or inequality. Isolate first. Then shield/tornado. Always expect two answers.
Open in QR engine
QR Trick #5
Pretend It's $100 high yield
Variable percent problems → substitute $100

Whatever the problem describes — car, stock, house — pretend it costs $100. Run the percentages on $100. Read the answer as a ratio or final value. The relationships stay the same regardless of original price. Eliminates the abstract paralysis of variable-laden percent problems.

When to useAny percent-change problem with variables instead of numbers. Also good for compound sequential percent changes.
Open in QR engine
QR Trick #6
Work-Rate Trap DAT trap
Rates add. Times never add.

If A finishes in 4 hr and B in 6 hr, their combined rate is 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12 per hour. Combined time = 12/5 = 2.4 hr. Averaging the times (5 hr) is wrong every time. Drain = negative rate: 1/6 inlet − 1/9 drain = 1/18 net.

Rate = 1/time. Add rates. Flip back: time = 1/rate.
When to useAny two-worker or pipe/drain problem. Convert to per-hour rates first, always.

[H+] ≈ sqrt(Ka · C) · [OH-] ≈ sqrt(Kb · C)

5% Rule
If x/C < 5%, drop the x
If x/C < 5% → C − x ≈ C (drop the x)
When to useAfter using the square root shortcut — verify the approximation is valid. If x > 5% of C, you must use the quadratic.

pH + pOH = pKw DAT trap
14 is a privilege, not a right
pH + pOH = pKw · = 14 only at 25°C
When to useAt body temp (37°C), Kw rises and pH + pOH ≈ 13.6. The DAT sometimes flips temperature. Don't assume 14.

Pure Water [H+]
Pure water — equal H and OH
[H+] = sqrt(Kw) → 10-7 at 25°C
When to usePure water [H+] = [OH-]. Heat the water → both rise; pH drops, but it's still neutral. Trap: students think heating makes water acidic.

GC #06 · Acid-Base
Strong Acid/Base — No ICE Needed
Strong → [H+] = C directly
Strong acid: [H+] = C · Strong base: [OH-] = C

Ka × Kb = Kw
Conjugate strengths trade off
Ka · Kb = Kw · pKa + pKb = 14 (at 25°C)
When to useGiven Ka, find Kb of the conjugate base instantly. Strong acid has an ultra-weak conjugate base (Kb near 0).

GC #08 · Acid-Base
pH Logic Shortcut
Direction first, then math
More H+ → pH decreases · More OH- → pH increases

GC #09 · Acid-Base
Amphoteric Species
Can both donate AND accept H+

H2O, HCO3-, HSO4-, H2PO4-, HPO42-, amino acids. Acts as a base in acidic solutions, as an acid in basic ones. Amphoteric is not the same as amphiprotic, but for DAT purposes they overlap.

When to useAny "which species can act as both acid and base?" question. Know the stock list cold.

Ksp to Solubility Forms high yield
Memorize the three forms

GC #14 · Ksp
Common-Ion Effect
Common ion kills solubility

Adding a shared ion shifts equilibrium left, dramatically reducing solubility. AgCl in 0.1 M NaCl dissolves ~10,000 times less than in pure water. Le Chatelier shifts left to oppose the added ion.

When to useAny Ksp problem where a salt is dissolved in a solution already containing one of its ions.

Equilibrium Shortcuts
GC #15 · Equilibrium
Q vs K Direction high yield
Q tells direction, K tells destination
Q < K → shift right (toward products)
Q > K → shift left (toward reactants)
Q = K → at equilibrium
When to useAny "which way does the reaction go?" question. Compute Q with given concentrations, compare to K.

GC #16 · Equilibrium
Le Chatelier — Concentration
System replaces what you take
+reactant → shift right · +product → shift left · remove → fills in

Removing a precipitate drives forward. Adding a pure solid has no effect (activity = 1). Diluting shifts toward more moles of gas.

When to useLe Chatelier perturbation questions. Read what's being added or removed, then apply the rule.

Temperature Changes K DAT trap
Only temperature changes K itself
Endo: heat = reactant → heat shifts right (K rises)
Exo: heat = product → heat shifts left (K falls)
When to useConcentration/pressure changes shift Q only. Temperature is the ONLY thing that changes K. Big trap question on the DAT.

Bonus · Cell / Biotech
CRISPR & Gene Editing
sgRNA = address · Cas9 = scissors

Cas9 nuclease guided by a single guide RNA (sgRNA) cuts double-stranded DNA at a complementary target. The PAM sequence (NGG for SpCas9) is the recognition signal. NHEJ = error-prone, knocks out genes. HDR + template = precise knock-in.

When to useModern parallel to restriction enzymes. Know sgRNA = address, Cas9 = scissors, NHEJ vs HDR.

Bonus · Genetics
Aneuploidies & Nondisjunction
Meiosis I error → all 4 gametes; Meiosis II → 2 of 4

Failure of homologs (meiosis I) or sister chromatids (meiosis II) to separate. Trisomy 21 = Down syndrome. XXY = Klinefelter. XO = Turner syndrome. Maternal age increases nondisjunction risk.

When to useAny nondisjunction mechanism or chromosome anomaly question.

Bonus · Cell
Apoptosis vs. Necrosis
Apoptosis = clean (caspases) · Necrosis = messy (inflammation)

Apoptosis: programmed, caspases, cell shrinks, DNA fragments into 200 bp ladders, membrane blebs but stays intact. Intrinsic path: mitochondrial cytochrome c release. Extrinsic: Fas/FasL death receptor. Bcl-2 inhibits; Bax promotes. Necrosis: unprogrammed, membrane ruptures, inflammation.

Bonus · Immunology
Innate vs. Adaptive Immunity
MHC I → CD8 · MHC II → CD4

Innate: fast, generic (barriers, phagocytes, complement, NK cells). Adaptive: slow, specific — B cells (antibodies/humoral) and T cells (cytotoxic CD8 + helper CD4, cell-mediated). Memory cells → faster secondary response. MHC I on all nucleated cells presents to CD8. MHC II on APCs presents to CD4.

Bonus · Microbiology
Viral Life Cycles
Lytic = kills · Lysogenic = latent · Retrovirus = RT

Lytic: rapid replication, lyses host. Lysogenic: viral DNA integrates as prophage, replicates with host until lytic induction. Retroviruses (HIV): ssRNA(+), uses reverse transcriptase to write RNA → DNA into host genome. RT and integrase are stock DAT material.

p53 gates G1/S · lost in >50% of cancers

Bonus · Genetics / Population
Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium
2pq = carrier frequency · q² = recessive frequency
p + q = 1 · p² + 2pq + q² = 1

Holds when no mutation, no selection, no migration, no drift, random mating. q² is often the only observable frequency. Deviations from the 5 conditions = evolution.

When to use"What fraction are carriers?" → 2pq. Start by finding q from the recessive frequency.

Bonus · Ecology
Trophic Levels & Energy Transfer
~10% passes to each level · energy flows · nutrients cycle

~10% of energy passes from one trophic level to the next (5-20% actual range); the rest is lost as heat. Pyramids of energy never invert. Pyramids of biomass and numbers can (parasites). Top predators are rare for thermodynamic reasons.

Plant Basics
C4 = spatial sep · CAM = temporal sep · alternation of generations
