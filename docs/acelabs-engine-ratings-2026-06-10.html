The one thing to fix first

There is a beautiful, colorblind-safe design system already in the repo — shared/al-tokens.css (Okabe-Ito correctness palette, Playfair/DM Sans type, teal+gold brand) and shared/al-motion.css (which honors a student's "reduce motion" setting). Only OChem and Gen Chem load it. QR and Bio do not — so they drift off-palette and, more seriously, their animations ignore students who get motion-sick or anxious from movement. Wiring all four engines to the shared tokens and motion stylesheet is a half-day job that instantly raises the floor on every screen a student sees.

*Bio's index.html is only a hub; its interactives live across 14 separate teaching pages (bio-physiology.html, bio-cell.html…). That fragmentation is itself the finding — the visual experience is governed page-by-page rather than by one system.

What's genuinely excellent

  • The Okabe-Ito correctness palette (--al-confirmed / --al-needs-review / --al-almost) is colorblind-safe — roughly 1 in 12 male students can't reliably tell red from green, and this design already solves it. Most prep tools never do.
  • Reduced-motion handling in al-motion.css collapses every animation to 0.01ms for students who set it — anxiety- and vestibular-aware, and rare in this market.
  • A real type system — Playfair Display for serif authority, DM Sans for body, JetBrains Mono for data — loaded consistently even where the tokens aren't.

What undercuts it for students

  • Two of four engines ignore the system. A student moving QR → OChem feels two different products: different button weights, different greens, different motion behavior.
  • Reduced-motion is unevenly honored. Because QR and Bio don't load al-motion.css, their animations play regardless of the student's OS setting.
  • ARIA coverage collapses outside OChem. A screen-reader or keyboard student gets a first-class experience in OChem and a thin one elsewhere.

What makes it feel best-in-class
  • Hand-drawn, animated mechanism diagrams — curved electron-pushing arrows that move frame by frame with synchronized narration. This is the thing students screenshot and send to friends.
  • Tier 1/2/3 progressive disclosure with <details> keeps the first screen calm — a student sees only the DAT-essential layer and opens depth on demand. This is the single best anti-overwhelm pattern in the build.
  • Predict-the-step commit cards make the student act before the reveal — the screen physically waits for a choice.
  • Accessibility is first-class: 202 ARIA hooks, reduced-motion honored, colorblind-safe outcome colors.
To reach 9.5
  • The file is ~31k lines in one HTML document — fast for students but heavy to load on a weak phone connection. Consider lazy-loading the lab sub-tools.
  • A few decorative unicode glyphs still slip past the no-emoji house style in chair-conformation labels — replace with the text+palette treatment used everywhere else.

07How to make it the best tool your students have

Sequenced by student-impact-per-hour. The first three are days, not weeks, and lift every screen at once.

1
Put all four engines on the shared design system

Add al-tokens.css + al-motion.css to QR and to all 14 Bio pages; replace inline hex with token variables. Outcome: one palette, one type voice, one motion contract — and reduced-motion finally honored everywhere. ~1 day · lifts every screen

2
Make accessibility uniform at OChem's level

Port the ARIA pattern from OChem (202 hooks) to GC's Recognition Lab + Lewis builder, QR's diagnostic, and Bio's drag interactives. Add click-to-place fallbacks for every drag. Outcome: keyboard and screen-reader students get a first-class experience in all four, and the colorblind-safe palette is applied consistently. ~3 days

3
Turn every "Off." into a teaching moment

Rewrite GC's ~170 stub feedback strings (and the Bio mock stubs) so a wrong answer shows the specific misconception, not a closed door. Visually, pair each with the --al-almost amber state rather than hard red so a near-miss feels recoverable, not punishing. ~1 week

4
Spread the two signature visual patterns

OChem's progressive-disclosure (Tier 1/2/3) and commit-before-reveal cards are the calmest, most active surfaces in the build. Bring progressive disclosure to Bio's dense nodes and GC's worked examples; bring commit-before-reveal to every Bio interactive (drag → predict → reveal). ~2 weeks

5
Add the assessment visuals the DAT actually uses

Students never see a figure/graph/table item in the mocks today. Add chart-reading items (Bio pedigrees and experiment graphs, GC titration curves, QR data tables) — both higher DAT fidelity and more visually engaging than text stems. ~2 weeks

6
Make progress visible and mobile-first

Surface a consistent progress/streak/mastery ribbon across all four engines (QR's digest is the template), and audit every multi-column table and diagram at 375px width. The tool a student opens on their phone at 11pm has to feel as finished as the desktop one. ongoing

The bottom line for your students

OChem already proves the ceiling: animated diagrams, calm progressive disclosure, commit-before-reveal, and full accessibility in one coherent skin. The work isn't inventing a better look — it's propagating the look you already built to the three engines that haven't caught up, and replacing punishing "Off." dead-ends with feedback that shows the way forward. Do the first three steps and every student, on every device, with any accessibility need, gets the OChem-grade experience in all four subjects.
