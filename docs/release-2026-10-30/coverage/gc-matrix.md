# General Chemistry coverage matrix (GC_SUBTOPICS, 64 subtopics)

Date: 2026-10-30. Repos read: `/home/user/acelabs` (read only, nothing edited) and `/home/user/AceDatPortal/src/scheduling/curriculum/subtopics.js`. Every count below was computed by the scripts in `coverage/scripts/` (listed at the end); nothing was counted by eye.

## Answer in one paragraph

Not yet. Of the 64 canonical GC subtopics, **28 are FULL, 27 PARTIAL, 7 THIN and 2 MISSING** under the five-criterion definition. The FULL ones are the classic calculation topics (moles, limiting reagent, gas laws, IMFs, pH, Ka/Kb, buffers, titration, K, Le Chatelier, enthalpy, entropy, Gibbs, rate laws, galvanic cells, Nernst and the structure topics). The holes are concrete: no question anywhere asks a student to balance an ordinary equation or classify a reaction type; fission, fusion and binding energy are taught in the engine `nuclear` module but have **zero** questions; rate factors (surface area, concentration, collision theory) have zero questions and only passing teaching; Beer's law and reaction types have no teaching at all; resonance, the three lab separations and polyprotic acids are taught only on the OChem side or in passing. Separately, the subtopic list itself misses six areas the ADA outline names (lab equipment and error analysis, the laws of thermodynamics, descriptive periodic chemistry, solubility factors, acid and base strength, and photons and atomic spectra), so even 64 out of 64 FULL would not be full coverage of the DAT. And FULL here means the raw material exists. None of the 64 has the five-step lesson that `docs/ace-system-decisions.md` specifies, and only 209 of 1609 served GC question records are shown with any visual (Recognition Lab 62, Retold scene drawings 98, Periodic Trends 41, trick-video checks 8); none of the 627 engine, 517 pool, 120 full-mock or 100 Climb GC items has one.

## How each criterion was measured

- **1 Teaching unit**: hand-mapped from the Gen Chem engine (`tools/gc/index.html`, 37 content modules: pattern text, formula, worked and faded examples) and GChem, Retold (`tools/gchem/index.html`, 14 nights x 7 scenes), with OChem units credited where the GC list files an OChem topic (lab separations, resonance). `yes` = a unit dedicated to the subtopic; `part` = taught only inside another unit, in one brief scene, or only on the OChem side; `no` = nothing. Keyword hits in every unit were computed (`teach-map.js`) before the hand mapping. The modules were spot-read, not proofread line by line. Note: 67 of 436 engine worked-example steps have an empty reason (`empty-why.txt`), concentrated in quantum-numbers (9 of 9), electrolysis (10 of 12), hybridization (6 of 8), calorimetry (5 of 8), eq-kc (4 of 6) and heating-curve (4 of 8).
- **2 Visual or tool**: `yes` = an interactive engine visual for it (35 of the 37 content modules have a visual with controls; Lab Techniques shows the static `lab-tech-table`, and Solubility Rules renders `word-translate`, the QR word-problem dictionary), a Retold scene SVG drawn for it, Recognition Lab stimuli, the Periodic Trends page, Lewis Sandbox, or an animated explainer (TikTok or trick video; the trick videos are silent today). `part` = only a shared or misfit visual.
- **3 Questions**: every served GC record (1609 records in 10 banks: GC engine 627, DAT mock pool GC 517, The Climb GC 100, full mocks GC 120, GChem Retold checks 98, Recognition Lab 62, Periodic Trends drill 41, engine diagnostic 33, trick-video checks 8, elimination drill 3; the 90 Prometric GC items are pool copies and are counted only as test appearances) was classified into one subtopic by `gc-rules.js` (tag-constrained keyword rules). For the four lab-separation rows, 96 OChem-bank lab items were classified the same way, because the GC list files those OChem topics under GC. Copies and rewrites were collapsed to one underlying problem (pool `GC_x` = engine `x`, Prometric = pool, full-mock ids traced to pool or engine; 495 of 517 pool items and 85 of 120 full-mock items are rewrites). **DAT-style** = the full-mock composer's exam-register rule (`scripts/compose-full-mocks.mjs` line 122: 6+ words, no `x=y` shorthand) plus 4 or 5 choices; the real DAT uses 5, so the 5-choice count is shown too. `yes` = 8+ DAT-style problems, `part` = 3 to 7, `no` = under 3.
- **Classifier accuracy**: hand audit of 140 items. Two tuning samples (60 + 40) found 11 misses; 9 were fixed by rule changes and 2 were left as neighbour calls (an ICE-style setup without the word ICE, an HF boiling-point item). A held-out sample of 40 then agreed on 36 (90%); 3 of its 4 misses were patched afterwards. Every miss was a neighbour in the same category (weak-acid pH filed under pH, a heating-curve problem under phase diagrams), so category totals are firmer than single-subtopic totals. Samples: `scripts/audit-sample*.txt`.
- **4 Explanations**: the best DAT-style version of each problem, using the census levels: `real` = 25+ words (short paragraph or detailed), `detailed` = also reasons about every distractor, `one line` = under 25 words. `yes` = 8+ problems with a real explanation, `part` = 1 to 7.
- **5 Tests**: appearances in the 4 full-length mocks (`tools/mock/data/full-mocks.js`, 30 GC each) and the 3 Prometric-style forms (`shared/dat-mock-tests.js`, 30 GC each), 210 GC items in all, against an expected share. The ADA publishes no per-topic weights (`tools/gc/research/BLUEPRINT_VERIFICATION.md`, from the 2026 Candidate Guide), so each of the 13 ADA areas is given an equal 16.2 items and each subtopic an equal share of its area. `yes` = at least half the expected count and at least one pool item (`diagnostic.html` draws 15 GC at random from the 517 pool items, with no topic stratification, so a subtopic with no pool item can never appear in the diagnostic). For the lab rows the appearances are in the OChem sections of those tests.
- **Rating**: FULL = all five `yes`. MISSING = no teaching, no visual, no question. THIN = under 3 underlying problems, or no dedicated teaching or visual and under 3 DAT-style problems. PARTIAL = everything else.

Bank codes in the questions column: E = Gen Chem engine bank, P = DAT mock pool, C = The Climb, F = full mocks, R = GChem Retold checks, RL = Recognition Lab, T = Periodic Trends drill, D = engine diagnostic, V = trick-video checks, X = elimination drill, OC... = OChem banks (lab rows only).

## Check of the subtopic list against the ADA outline

**Source.** `www.ada.org` and `www.kaptest.com` do not resolve from this sandbox (DNS blocked), so the Candidate Guide PDF could not be re-fetched today; these hosts would have to be allowed in the session network settings. The check uses the repo transcription of the ADA *2026 DAT Candidate Guide* (fetched 2026-05-07, PDF dated 12/11/2025) and the *DAT User Guide* (April 2025) in `tools/gc/research/BLUEPRINT_VERIFICATION.md`, cross-checked against public search snippets (Kaplan, Chad's Prep, Union Test Prep outlines) that list the same 13 areas. GC is 30 of the 100 Survey of Natural Sciences items; the ADA publishes no per-area counts; the GC outline has not changed since at least 2019.

The 13 ADA GC areas and their named contents: (1) Stoichiometry and General Concepts: percent composition, empirical formulae, balancing equations, moles and molecular formulas, molar mass, density, calculations from balanced equations. (2) Gases: kinetic molecular theory, Dalton, Boyle, Charles, ideal gas law. (3) Liquids and Solids: intermolecular forces, phase changes, vapor pressure, structures, polarity, properties. (4) Solutions: polarity, colligative and non-colligative properties, forces, concentration calculations. (5) Acids and Bases: pH, strength, Bronsted-Lowry reactions, calculations. (6) Chemical Equilibria: molecular, acid/base, precipitation, calculations, Le Chatelier. (7) Thermodynamics and Thermochemistry: laws of thermodynamics, Hess's law, spontaneity, enthalpies and entropies, heat transfer. (8) Chemical Kinetics: rate laws, activation energy, half-life. (9) Oxidation-Reduction Reactions: balancing, oxidation numbers, electrochemical calculations and concepts. (10) Atomic and Molecular Structure: electron configuration, orbital types, Lewis-dot diagrams, atomic theory, quantum theory, molecular geometry, bond types, sub-atomic particles. (11) Periodic Properties: representative elements, transition elements, periodic trends, descriptive chemistry. (12) Nuclear Reactions: balancing equations, binding energy, decay processes, particles, terminology. (13) Laboratory: basic techniques, equipment, error analysis, safety, data analysis.

### The 2026 Organic Chemistry change and the list

The ADA updated the OChem examination specifications for administrations from about April 2026 (repo note: "Updated 08/13/2025"; public reporting from Booster, Bootcamp and SDN). Curved-arrow mechanisms are now an explicit category with single versus combined mechanisms and reaction coordinate diagrams, the topic names were simplified, and the weighting shifted slightly toward acid-base and mechanisms; the ADA says this is not a major content change. It does not touch General Chemistry. The GC list does not need changes for it. The OC list partly reflects it: `oc_arrow_pushing`, `oc_reaction_energy` and `oc_thermo_kinetic` (category "Reaction Mechanisms & Energetics", 3 subtopics), and the file header says the 2026 update was cross-checked. It has no explicit "single vs combined mechanism" or "fishhook / radical arrow" subtopic, which the Ace Labs 2026-format set already tests (17 combined-mechanism items, 17 fishhook items in `tools/ochem/bank/ochem-2026-pool.js`). That gap belongs to the OC section audit.
