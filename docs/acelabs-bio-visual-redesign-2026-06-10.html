Ace Labs · Bio Visual Redesign Theory

Can every Bio visual be top tier?
Yes — and here is the theory for each one.

A research-grounded redesign brief for every interactive and diagram in the Bio engine. The core problem is not ugliness — it is that ~36 visuals run on four reused interaction templates, so concepts with very different deep structure all get the same drag-bin, slider, or click-to-reveal. This document sets out an evidence base from the learning sciences, derives a taxonomy that matches each concept's deep structure to a distinct visual grammar, and then specifies the top-tier replacement for every existing visual — starting with the body-systems map, which today is a placeholder silhouette.

00The thesis

A visual is "top tier" when its grammar matches the concept's deep structure and it forces the student to generate rather than watch. Both halves are settled science. The Bio engine already nails neither consistently — not because the team lacks skill, but because bespoke visuals are expensive to hand-author, so four templates got stretched over everything. Remove that constraint (which is exactly what an AI author removes) and every visual can clear the bar.

The one-sentence rule

Pick the visual grammar from the concept's deep structure — cycle, gradient, network, hierarchy, mechanism, distribution, spatial map, or comparison — then wrap it in a predict → manipulate → see-the-real-result → self-explain loop, and strip everything that doesn't carry meaning.

01The evidence base

Six findings from the learning sciences drive every recommendation below. These are not preferences; each has strong empirical support.

  • Spatial contiguity wins, hugely. Words belong on the structure they label, not in a caption far away. Supported in 22 of 22 tests, median effect size ~1.10 — one of the largest in education. (Mayer; Mayer & Fiorella)
  • Segment and pause; don't autoplay. Free-running animation suffers the transience effect — information vanishes before it's processed. System-paced steps (advance on click, one idea per step) beat both autoplay and pure learner-control, because novices don't know when to pause. (Spanjers et al.; transience research)
  • Animation only beats a static diagram when motion is the thing being learned, and only if it obeys the apprehension + congruence principles. For structure and relationships, a signposted static or step-revealed diagram is usually better and lighter. (animation-vs-static meta-analyses)
  • Generative activity is the multiplier. Drawing, predicting, self-explaining, and enacting produce better comprehension and transfer than studying a finished figure — and the quality of what the student generates predicts their test performance. (Fiorella & Mayer, generative learning)
  • Interactive simulations out-teach lectures and demos when built on implicit scaffolding — affordances invite exploration, real-time results reward it, text is minimal, and the design is iterated on real student interviews. (PhET Interactive Simulations)
  • For spatial/anatomical content, color-coded schematic 3D beats photorealism. Realism raises cognitive load (especially for lower-spatial students); color-coding raises performance with no load penalty. (Anatomical Sciences Education; Scientific Reports)

02Eight visual grammars — matched to deep structure

The antidote to "same format over and over" is to stop choosing the format by convenience and start choosing it by the shape of the idea. Eight grammars cover all of DAT Bio. Each is visually distinct, so a student always knows what kind of thinking a screen is asking for.

Cyclic dialloop / phase
A radial loop the student steps around, with the current phase lit and the driver of the next transition called out. For: cardiac cycle, menstrual cycle, cell cycle, Krebs, Calvin.
Gradient field2-D continuum
A heatmap/contour the student builds, watching a quantity intensify across space. For: nephron countercurrent, O₂ unloading, membrane potential, resource clines.
Routing networknodes + decisions
A node-link graph where a token flows and branches; wrong branches visibly fail. For: immune response, signal transduction, energy flow up a food web, reflex arcs.
Living treenesting / hierarchy
A collapsible hierarchy the student assembles; each branch reveals the trait that defines it. For: phylogeny, taxonomy, classification, gene-family trees.
Mechanism playerordered motion
A step-gated process; each step asks the student to predict the next move before it plays. For: replication, transcription/translation, sliding filament, action potential, mitosis.
Live distributionstatistics / probability
A histogram or curve recomputed from parameters the student sets — the math is real, not staged. For: Hardy-Weinberg, selection, Punnett probabilities, population growth.
Zoomable mapspatial anatomy
A color-coded schematic with semantic zoom; predict-before-reveal on each structure's job. For: body systems, heart, organelle atlas, brain regions.
Synced comparatorcontrast
Two views locked together with a difference-highlighter that pulses what changed. For: mitosis vs meiosis, innate vs adaptive, stem-cell potency, cytoskeleton filaments, prokaryote vs eukaryote.

03The universal interaction spine

Whatever the grammar, the same four-beat loop turns a picture into a learning event. This is where generative learning, segmentation, and implicit scaffolding meet.

  1. Predict. Before anything moves or reveals, the student commits — drag, choose, or sketch. (Generative + commit-before-reveal; this is OChem's best move, absent in most Bio visuals.)
  2. Manipulate. One affordance, real-time response, minimal text. The student changes a variable and the visual answers immediately. (PhET implicit scaffolding.)
  3. See the real result. The output is computed, not staged — the Hill curve actually shifts, the allele frequency actually drifts, the gradient actually multiplies. Trust comes from the model being real.
  4. Self-explain. A short "why did that happen?" the student answers, graded with targeted feedback. (Self-explanation effect; the quality of the explanation predicts transfer.)

Segmentation rule: the loop is system-paced — the student advances step by step, never a slider they can blow past. Spatial-contiguity rule: every label sits on its structure. Coherence rule: the decorative 800×450 letterbox frame and gradient backgrounds go — they add load, not meaning.

05The flagship: the body-systems map

Today it is an outline silhouette with the copy "Body Systems Map flagship coming." It is the right thing to build first, because it is the front door to all of physiology and it currently undersells the whole engine.

What top tier looks like here
  • Grammar: Zoomable map. A clean, color-coded schematic human (not photorealistic — realism raises load for exactly the anxious, lower-spatial students this product is built for), one accent per system from the existing Okabe-Ito palette.
  • Semantic zoom. Tap cardiovascular → the body recedes and the heart-and-vessels schematic comes forward with its one signature animation (a single beat), a "drill into the cardiac cycle" route, and three highest-yield facts placed on the structures (spatial contiguity).
  • Predict-before-reveal. Each system opens with one question — "If this organ stopped, what's the first thing that fails?" — answered before the overview unlocks. Generative, and it sets the diagnostic hook.
  • It is the router. Every other physiology visual (cardiac dial, nephron gradient, AP player, ventilation, endocrine network) is reachable in one tap from its organ, so the map is the spatial index of the whole hub — fixing the current "stack of separate pages" feel.
  • Coherence pass. No decorative gradient backdrop, no letterbox frame; the silhouette earns its pixels or they're cut.

Result: the screen that today says "coming soon" becomes the most memorable surface in Bio and the navigational spine of physiology — the difference between "laughably bad" and the thing a student opens first.

06Can everything become top tier?

Yes — and the work is bounded

Every Bio concept maps cleanly to one of eight grammars, and every grammar takes the same four-beat generative spine. Nothing here needs a research breakthrough; it needs bespoke authoring at a volume that was previously uneconomic. Roughly a third of the visuals (action potential, clade builder, cardiac dial, Hardy-Weinberg, central dogma) are already at or near top tier and only need the predict/self-explain beat added. A third need a grammar swap (nephron, immune, domain sorter, endocrine, Krebs). A third need to be computed-for-real or built fresh (Bohr/Hill, selection histogram, molecular techniques, the body map).

Where Fable 5 is the unlock. The reason these are templates today is that hand-authoring 36 bespoke, accessible, scientifically-verified interactives is expensive. That is precisely the constraint an AI author removes: Fable 5 can generate each visual's SVG, its step-gated interaction, the real model behind it (Hill equation, allele-frequency dynamics, countercurrent multiplication), the predict-and-self-explain copy in Riya's voice, and the keyboard/ARIA fallbacks — then verify the biology against the teaching pages. The theory in this document is the spec; producing it at quality across all 36 is now a tractable build, not a multi-year art project.

Suggested first build

The nephron countercurrent gradient field — it is the worst format-concept mismatch, it teaches the exact thing the DAT asks and the current slider cannot, and it proves the "gradient field" grammar that several other visuals (Krebs, O₂ unloading) will reuse. Ship it next to the existing version and the difference between "templated" and "top tier" becomes self-evident.
