How it teaches —
where the science is loose and where the teaching breaks

Four parallel reviewers, one per engine, each working in the combined voice of a PhD subject expert and a veteran DAT tutor. They sampled questions, distractors, explanations, mechanism narration, interactive scaffolds, diagnostics, and the spaced-retrieval design. This document is the synthesis — cross-cutting patterns, must-fix bugs, per-engine critiques, and an actionable plan to lift the teaching from "above market" to "the best in this category."

The headline pattern

Across all four engines, the same shape recurs: architecture and pedagogical structure are tutor-grade; question-bank execution and active-learning depth lag behind. The system knows how to teach — the bug is that too many surfaces still tell instead of make the student do. Fix the bank-level execution and the engine moves from "the most thoughtful DAT prep on the market" to the genuinely-best-in-class.

01Seven patterns that recur in every engine

These are the cross-cutting findings — fix any one of them in one engine and you have a template for fixing it in the other three.

1. Recognition dressed up as retrieval

Retrieval cards re-state the explanation paragraph immediately above. The Bio gastrulation node teaches "heart from mesoderm" in coach notes, then asks "heart and skeletal muscle derive from?" 30 lines later. QR Pattern Library asks "what fraction equals 0.625?" instead of "0.625 × 24 = ?". This is recognition, not transfer — and the DAT tests transfer.

2. "Then magic" worked examples

Worked examples advertised as step-by-step are often 2-3 lines of bullet points with a one-phrase "why." GC's Gibbs keystone worked example is one bullet: "(−,+) → always." OChem mechanisms outside the three predict-the-step ones can be skimmed Next-Next-Next with no commit. The reasoning chain is asserted, not modeled.

3. Distractors that don't teach

Many wrong-answer diag fields are single-word stubs: "Off.", "Wrong.", "Reversed." A student who picks that answer learns nothing about which misconception they fell into. GC has hundreds of these; Bio has them in the mock; even OChem's strong items have a few throwaways. Every "Off." is a missed teaching moment.

4. Mechanism without the "why it has to be this way"

Bio teaches polymerase only adds to 3'-OH without explaining the dNTP triphosphate energy that forces the rule. The loop-of-Henle node teaches limb-by-limb permeability without ever explaining countercurrent multiplication, which is the whole point. The Bohr effect explains rightward shift without why hemoglobin evolved that allostery. Mechanism becomes memorized when it should be inevitable.

5. Diagnostics with silent skills

The Bio Pulse Check covers 24 nodes, leaving most of physiology and devo unprobed. GC's 25-item diagnostic ignores 8 modules including IMFs, hybridization, calorimetry, colligative, titration. QR's diagnostic never probes any of the Productive-Failure-eligible modules (mixture, work-rate, p-cond, harm, interest). Students get routed into PF on topics the diagnostic literally has no signal on.

6. Calibration too flat for the top scorers

Difficulty hovers at DAT 19-21 across mocks. A 40-question bank at one difficulty does not differentiate a 20 from a 24. The DAT's hardest items are stratified — multi-step transfer, figure/graph reading, integration across two science sections. None of the mocks currently have figure/table items.

7. The killer feature exists in one place — and is missing everywhere else

Each engine has at least one piece of genuinely best-in-market pedagogy that nothing else in the build replicates. OChem's predict-the-step (commit-before-reveal) on SN2/EAS/acetal is the highest-evidence active-learning move in the whole product — implemented for 3 of 14 mechanisms. QR's pacing-sim Two-Pass Drill forces 5 skips per session — implemented in an orphan iframe, not on the main page. Bio's misconception-tagged repair patterns are the cleanest design — but the diag fields don't tag back to them. GC's misconception taxonomy has 77 entries but no item references them. The patterns work; the wiring across the rest of the engine doesn't exist yet.

These are not stylistic gripes. Each one teaches the wrong content or breaks an explanation panel. Listed by file:line for direct action.

OChemtools/ochem/index.html:10776

Hydroboration Frame 1 — directional reversal teaches wrong chemistry

Step 1 narration says "B-H bond swings to the less hindered carbon (C1)." That's backwards. Boron goes to C1; H goes to C2 (the more substituted carbon). It's the entire point of anti-Markovnikov hydroboration, and the hub contradicts itself — Step 3 of the same mechanism says "Step 1 delivered H to C2." A careful student will catch the inconsistency. Fix both the narration and the SVG arrow label.

03Biology — content & pedagogy

Above the typical DAT-prep median for both content correctness and pedagogical structure. The weakness is assessment design and recognition-vs-transfer.

Strengths to preserve
  • The Trap / Rule / Move triad on every node is a real pedagogical innovation in this market. Most DAT prep is undifferentiated prose.
  • The Coach notes on Hardy-Weinberg, the immune cell summary, and the Bohr-effect respiratory writeup are tutor-quality field notes.
  • The repair-pattern registry (bio-registry.js:147-196) — 12 trap pairs each tied to a node — is exactly how a tutor thinks.
  • The misses → Ace Cards pipeline connects assessment to study time; the right spaced-retrieval architecture.
  • Build-mode interactives (replication fork, mitosis scrubbers, cardiac cycle, fertilization stages) are real teaching tools, not decoration.
  • The "Stuck? Take a beat" trigger — anxiety-aware design that most DAT prep ignores entirely.
Pedagogical weaknesses
  • Retrieval cards re-state the explanation paragraph immediately above — the single biggest fix-it. Move them to a sibling page, disguise the stem, or force a free-text reflection step in between.
  • Mechanism without the "why it has to be this way." Add a one-paragraph "Why this exists" block to every Tier-1 node: replication directionality (energy is in the incoming dNTP), loop-of-Henle (the medulla has to be hyperosmotic to make hypertonic urine), Bohr effect (the same proton that flags "work happening here" pries O₂ off).
  • The "Move" subcard is procedural without judgment. Recipe-trained students freeze on a rotated figure or a circular plasmid. Each Move block needs a "what's the variable here?" check.
  • Build-mode tests sequencing, not consequences. After the replication-fork drag-and-drop, follow with "if primase is missing, what's the first thing that breaks?" — that's the DAT-style transfer question.
DAT-fit issues
  • Stems are textbook-quiz style, not DAT style. DAT Bio stems are almost always scenario-bound: clinical vignette, experiment, graph, pedigree. Rewrite ~30% of mock items as scenarios.
  • Difficulty is too flat. Stratify the mock as ~15 fundamentals, ~20 moderate, ~5 stretch with figure/table reading. Currently zero items require reading a figure.
  • The "fragile correct" mechanic is good in concept, broken in execution. Self-report bias; no "slow but correct" flag; students can ignore confidence buttons entirely. Make confidence required to advance, add a calibration tab to results.
  • Mock distribution over-weights Cell, under-weights Genetics. Bring Genetics to 10-12 items (~28%); shrink Cell to 10-12.
High-yield DAT topics that are weak or missing
  • Countercurrent multiplication in the loop of Henle — taught limb-by-limb, never as a system. This is what the DAT actually tests.
  • End-replication problem and telomerase — only mentioned in passing.
  • Restriction enzymes, PCR, gel electrophoresis — completely absent. The DAT tests molecular techniques every test.
  • RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone) — aldosterone alone is mentioned, never the upstream cascade.
  • Frank-Starling, lung volumes (TV, IRV, ERV, RV, VC, TLC) — not taught.
  • Hormone classification cross-table (steroid vs peptide vs amine + receptor type) — listed individually, never tabled.
  • Cori cycle and lactate metabolism — what happens to pyruvate anaerobically and how lactate is recycled.
"Insulin is secreted by the pancreas and acts to lower blood glucose by promoting cellular uptake."Bio diagnostic — recall stem
"After a high-carbohydrate meal, plasma glucose rises from 90 to 160 mg/dL. Which hormonal response best explains the return to baseline by 2 hours post-meal? (B) Insulin from β-cells, promoting GLUT4 translocation in muscle and adipose."DAT-style rewrite — same fact, transfer required

04Gen Chem — content & pedagogy

One of the better self-built DAT GC engines. Architecture is unusually thoughtful (adaptive diagnostic, mastery path, pattern → worked → faded → independent → mastered, DAT-weighted mock, four solid companion tools). But the bank itself sits at DAT 19-21 calibration, not the 22-25 it is aimed at, and a half-dozen items have errors serious enough to teach the wrong thing.

Strengths to preserve
  • The colligative module is the best-written content in the engine — distractors that name the specific misconception ("you used i = 3, which is CaCl₂, not NaCl"). Template for the rest.
  • VSEPR polarity decision tree — pedagogically excellent: "B3 — linear/tetrahedral/trigonal planar, symmetric" distractors that teach the cancellation principle.
  • Recognition Lab with 9-second timer and hand-authored SVGs for reaction coordinates, integrated rate-law plots, Q vs K bar charts. Real pattern-recognition training.
  • Lewis Sandbox per-molecule commonTrap field ("students see four hydrogens and assume CH₄ must be polar"). 18 molecules, 5-stage builder.
  • SIM_TOPIC_WEIGHTS well-calibrated to ADA distributions — stoich 17%, gas laws 6%, acid-base 12%, equilibrium 7%, thermo 10%, electrochem 7%, quantum 2%. Don't drift.
  • Pre-answer confidence rating in the diagnostic — captured before correctness is revealed, much harder to game than post-answer.
Pedagogical weaknesses
  • Worked examples for keystone Tier-1 modules are one-bullet reveals. Gibbs worked Q1 in full: {what:'(−,+) → always', why:'Both terms drive ΔG negative.'} Expand buffers, gibbs, entropy, rate-laws to 5+ steps with a mid-stream predict: challenge.
  • The 77-entry misconception taxonomy is unweaponized. Items don't tag misconceptionIds, so the system can never say "your dominant misconception cluster is eq.lechat.heat-as-reactant." Add the tags. Backfill the top 100 most-missed items first.
  • Many "coach tricks" aren't tricks. "Reverse → flip sign" is a fact. A trick answers what do I do when stuck?, what's the no-calc shortcut?, or what's the trap I should sanity-check against? Use vsepr's "all same, no LP" and ph-basics's "pH ≈ n − log(a)" as templates.
  • The "Off." / "Wrong." stub diag problem — hundreds of wrong-answer explanations are single-word. Equilibrium, buffer/titration, and galvanic/Nernst are the highest-priority cleanups because the wrong choice almost always corresponds to a named misconception.
DAT-fit issues
  • No-calculator preparedness is uneven. Several diff=3 items require calculator-grade arithmetic: ks-h1 takes ∛(2.75×10⁻¹³), x8 requires Arrhenius two-T logarithms. Sweep every diff=3 item for 90-second no-calc solvability.
  • Estimation as a skill is missing. No module teaches the tutor toolbox: log 2 ≈ 0.3, log 3 ≈ 0.48, "tenths of a pH unit per factor-of-2," "1 atm·L = 100 J." Build a Tier-3 "No-calc shortcuts" module — would be a real differentiator.
  • Mastery gate is 3-in-a-row from a small pool. For conceptual skills (Q vs K, Le Châtelier, buffer direction), this rewards item-pattern-matching. Mix in at least one cross-skill item per mastery probe.
High-yield DAT topics that are weak or missing
  • Solubility rules + net ionic equations + spectator ions — not a module. The DAT tests this consistently.
  • Oxidation-state assignment drill (Mn in MnO₄⁻, S in S₂O₃²⁻, Cr in Cr₂O₇²⁻) — one worked example exists; needs 2-3 drill items.
  • Electrochemistry concentration cells, half-life chained problems, phase-diagram point identification — under-built relative to DAT frequency.
  • Eight diagnostic-silent skills — IMFs, hybridization, calorimetry, electrolysis, heating curves, colligative, titration, red-pot — never probed. Add one item each to the diagnostic.

05Organic Chem — content & pedagogy

The strongest single-file hand-authored DAT OChem learning surface the reviewer has read. The pedagogical architecture is best-in-class; the gaps are bank coverage (no MCQs in stereo / nomenclature / spec / lab-techniques) and the predict-the-step coverage (3 of 14 mechanisms).

Strengths to preserve
  • Tier 1/2/3 collapse discipline. Hides depth behind <details>, surfaces only DAT-essential at Tier 1. The right architecture for a recognition-heavy 30-Q/30-min exam.
  • Mechanism animations explain WHY, not just what. "The leaving group leaves because…", "the nucleophile attacks because…" — consistent causal language across SN1, EAS-nitration, acetal, imine. Highest-leverage move in the engine.
  • Predict-the-step on SN2 / EAS / acetal. Commit-before-reveal active learning; the EAS Q3 ("Why is H₂SO₄ catalytic?") tests a concept not a step. Model implementations.
  • CARDIO 5-factor pKa decision walk. Replaces 30-value memorization with transfer-quality reasoning.
  • Alpha-condensation "Boss Plate" — "8 reactions, one skeleton, only the electrophile varies." Best-in-class meta-organization for a chapter students chronically silo.
  • Lab techniques as a real DAT topic — Tollens, Fehling, iodoform, AgNO₃, Br₂/CCl₄, Jones, each with a micro-experiment and trap callout. The part most prep tools botch is built correctly here.
  • Carbocation-rearrangement trap in E1 is taught with a worked 1,2-H shift — high-yield DAT content done right.
Pedagogical weaknesses
  • Predict-the-step covers 3 of 14 mechanisms. The other 11 can be skimmed Next-Next-Next. Build the next five in priority order:
    1. SN1 — racemization is the unique teach; contrast partner to SN2.
    2. E2 — anti-periplanar geometry + bulky-base Hofmann override.
    3. E1 dehydration — predict the 1,2-hydride shift before elimination.
    4. Imine — pH 4-5 bell curve, the unique DAT imine question.
    5. Aldol — enolate attack site + heat-driven E1cb dehydration.
  • Pattern-matching shortcut creep. Diels-Alder callout teaches "count double bonds in the product" without the why. Add a one-line tail: "because alkene = 1 π in, alkyne = 2 π in, and only the diene's two π become the new σ bonds."
  • Retrosynthesis is offered but barely taught as a skill. The reagent predictor has a retro mode; the synthesis-lab is forward-multi-step. Neither walks the student through the disconnection logic ("for a 1,3-dicarbonyl, disconnect at the α,β-bond"). Highest-yield mid-to-late DAT skill, underserved.
DAT-fit issues
  • Exam-sim has zero stereo, zero nomenclature, zero mass-spec, zero lab-techniques MCQs. Despite gorgeous hubs for all four. Even if the exam-sim sampling were stratified, those buckets are empty.
  • Exam-sim sampling is uniform random + biased shuffle — not weighted by DAT topic distribution. Adding stratification is half a day's work and a real win.
  • Per-hub breakdown shows "No data" on most hubs in any 20-question pull — students get told their weakest hub is the one they didn't see. Stratify with at least 1 from each hub.
  • Aromatic Q1 oversimplifies ortho/para statistics. "Para usually dominant" — actually for toluene nitration ortho wins numerically (~58:38). Tighten the explanation.
Bank gaps to fill (priority order)
  1. 5 stereo MCQs (R/S, meso, E/Z, enantiomer-vs-diastereomer, optical activity).
  2. 5 IR MCQs + 5 NMR MCQs (splitting/integration, match-the-spectrum).
  3. 5 mass-spec MCQs (M+2 Cl/Br, McLafferty, base-peak, fragment-loss).
  4. 5 lab-techniques MCQs (extraction logic, TLC Rf, qual-test ID, distillation choice).
  5. 5 nomenclature MCQs (spiro, bicyclic, E/Z, lowest-locant, multi-functional-group priority).
  6. Grignard + protic functional group trap, NaBH₄ vs LiAlH₄ scope, crossed aldol with benzaldehyde, Sandmeyer/diazonium.

06QR — content & pedagogy

A tutor-aware engine, not a generic question bank. The Sarah-mode summary, the right-subset Productive Failure scaffolds, the pacing-sim Two-Pass Drill, and the calculator-feasibility framing on exps are pedagogically real. The bugs are coverage holes and a few "pattern" labels on features that aren't actually patterns.

Strengths to preserve
  • The Sarah summary digest (6-row builder) compresses what a tutor would otherwise scroll logs for. Real workflow compression.
  • 5 of 8 Productive Failure problems are genuine PF — mixture, work-rate, harm, p-cond, count-pc. Distractors map to canonical misconceptions; the youDid block diagnoses which trap; the reveal contrasts failed approaches against the canonical move.
  • The pacing-sim Two-Pass Drill (proposals/agent-08-pacing) forces 5 skips per pass-1, throttled coach-whisper recommendations, lift-from-guessing debrief. Genuinely teaches the skip move; doesn't just measure it.
  • Calculator-feasibility on exps ("Can I plug this directly into the on-screen DAT calculator? Only square root, no exponent button. Rewrite until you can.") A+ thinking — exactly the right pedagogy for a 4-function-calculator test.
  • Pre-answer confidence rating in the diagnostic — much harder to game than post-answer ratings.
  • DINA-light scorer + Laplace smoothing + pruneDescendants — real psychometric thinking, not weight-the-misses heuristics.
Pedagogical weaknesses
  • Pattern Library recognition prompts are surface-feature lookups. "What fraction equals 0.625?" trains the dictionary; what you want trained is "0.625 × 24 = ?" in the middle of a stem. Drop the recognition prompts on the fraction-decimal chunks and double the generation drills.
  • 3 of 8 PF problems aren't really PF. discr (student needs the formula before they can productively fail), interest (gap is declarative not procedural), zscore (only one attack plan exists). Convert to instruction-first with "you'll fall into one of these three traps if you rush" framing.
  • Confidence data is collected but only half-used. The bucket scorer uses confWrong as a penalty; the new DINA-light scorer ignores confidence entirely. Two parallel scorers, one signal — pick a path.
  • The "specific answer is more likely correct" guessing heuristic at index.html:5629 is unsupported testlore. Drop or restrict to "if you've ruled out two and the remaining two differ by an order of magnitude, pick the one matching your ballpark."
DAT-fit issues
  • Mental-math estimation as a meta-skill is not trained. Estimation should be the check on every chunk output ("0.625 > 1/2, so the answer to 0.625 × 24 should be > 12"). Append a "ballpark check" Sarah trick to every Pattern Library chunk.
  • Distractors are too clean. Friday Mini-Mock Q22 (rectangle area = 72) offers [64, 81, 48] — the DAT would offer [60, 72, 84, 90] specifically to punish eyeballing. Add 1-2 ballpark-trap distractors per skill area.
  • Stem variety — pct-change items repeatedly use $ framing. Diversify (recipe scaling, dilution, lab dilutions).
Bank gaps to fill
  • Trig is essentially missing as a module. Three Pattern-Library values exist; SOHCAHTOA, sin²+cos²=1, inverse trig "what angle gives sin=1/2," unit-circle quadrant signs — none. Add a Tier-2 module.
  • Geometry formula bank is thin. No parallelogram, no trapezoid, no cylinder/cone/sphere SA-and-V table. Inflate geo-2d and vol-sa.
  • Data interpretation from charts/tables is absent. Real DAT QR has it; the engine has no chunk or module. Wave-2 add at minimum.
  • Probability without replacement — mentioned once, never drilled. Add a chunk.
  • Diagnostic doesn't probe any Tier-2 PF-eligible module (mixture, work-rate, p-cond, harm, interest). Six items closes the hole.

09The bottom line

What this product is, after the pedagogy lens

The same answer as the build audit, with more confidence: a supplemental, tutor-in-the-loop repair lab and pacing rehearsal with the rare property that the teaching architecture is already tutor-grade. The build is not held back by what it doesn't know how to do — every move it needs to make is already implemented somewhere in the product.
